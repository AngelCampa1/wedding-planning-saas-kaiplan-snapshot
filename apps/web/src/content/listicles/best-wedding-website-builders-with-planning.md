## Why Most Wedding Website Builders Stop at RSVP

Building a wedding website is a solved problem. Templates exist, RSVP flows are standard, and guest communication tools are well-understood. Every platform in this list does it adequately.

The planning tools are where they diverge.

A wedding website serves your guests. A planning tool serves you, the couple managing the logistics behind the wedding. These are different products with different users and different requirements, and they happen to be bundled together on most platforms because it's a natural funnel.

You build a website on The Knot. You use The Knot checklist. You find vendors through The Knot marketplace. The website is the entry point; the marketplace is the product.

## What "Planning Features" Actually Means Beyond RSVP

When evaluating a wedding website builder for planning depth, look beyond RSVP and guest list:

**Budget tracking**, is it a real ledger (vendor quotes, deposits, balances, due dates) or a category estimate tool? Almost all platforms offer the latter.

**Vendor management**, can you manage vendors you found yourself, or only vendors in the platform's marketplace? Can you store contract details, notes, and payment information?

**Seating chart**, does it connect to your RSVP data, or is it a separate tool requiring manual data entry?

**Checklist customization**, can you adjust the timeline for your actual wedding date, or is it a generic template?

Most website builders answer "basic" or "no" to at least three of these. The gap between what couples need and what bundled tools provide is why spreadsheets remain the backup planning tool for so many weddings.

## The Website-as-Funnel Problem

Platforms that bundle a free website with planning tools make money from vendor advertising. The website gets you in the door; the planning tools keep you there; the vendor marketplace is the revenue. This isn't a conspiracy, it's a rational business model.

The tradeoff is that planning features are designed to serve the vendor discovery funnel, not to give you the most useful planning experience. Budget tools show you average costs by category to help you understand what vendors cost. Vendor suggestions appear in your checklist next to the booking task. The tools and the marketplace reinforce each other.

Kaiplan doesn't earn from vendor advertising. The website and planning tools exist to help you plan your wedding, not to move you toward a marketplace booking. That's a different product philosophy, and it requires charging for the tool directly. Plans start at $20/mo or $100 lifetime.
