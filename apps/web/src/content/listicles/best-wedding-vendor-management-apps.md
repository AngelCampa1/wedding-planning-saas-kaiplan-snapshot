## What "vendor management" actually means

Most wedding apps use "vendor management" to mean a contact list. You enter your photographer's name and mark them as booked. That's the product.

Real vendor management means tracking the relationship across time: the initial quote, the signed contract and what it covers, the deposit you paid and when, the remaining balance and when it's due, any communication history that matters, and what you need from this vendor in the weeks before the wedding.

That is a more demanding product to build. Most wedding platforms built marketplaces — the discovery side of finding vendors. Managing those relationships once booked is an afterthought.

## The gap between discovery and management

When you find a caterer through The Knot or Zola, the marketplace did its job. When you sign a contract six weeks later and need to track a $2,000 deposit against a $8,500 total quote with a final payment due October 1, you've moved past what those platforms were built to support.

The tools that solve this — Aisle Planner, HoneyBook — are built for the professionals who manage weddings for a living. That is not a coincidence. Professionals have enough vendors and enough weddings that building a proper management system is worth the time and cost. For a couple planning one wedding, the calculus is different.

## A practical approach

For most self-planning couples, the most functional vendor management system is: a spreadsheet with payment data (the [free budget template](/free/budget-template) covers this) and a Google Drive folder per vendor with contracts. Reference the Drive links from your spreadsheet or planning app notes. Kaiplan connects the payment tracking side to your vendor records so you don't need two systems for that part. Contract storage is still Google Drive.
