## Before You Talk to Any Vendor: The Baseline Questions

These apply to every vendor category. Ask them first, before you get into the specifics of their craft.

**Availability and logistics:**

- Are you available on our date? (Obvious, but confirm before investing time in the conversation.)
- How many events do you book per weekend? Per day?
- Will you personally be at our wedding, or will it be a team member? If a team member, can we meet them before signing?
- What time will you arrive and what time will you leave?
- What happens if you are sick or have an emergency on our wedding day? What is your backup plan?

**Money:**

- What is your total fee, including all taxes and service charges?
- What requires an additional fee beyond your base package? (This is where hidden costs live.)
- What is your deposit amount and when is it due?
- What is your cancellation and refund policy?
- Do prices change if we need to adjust the timeline or scope after signing?

**Contract specifics:**

- Can we see a sample contract before committing?
- What is and is not included in the contract? (Get specifics. "Full day coverage" can mean 6 hours or 12.)
- How far in advance of the wedding do you need final details (timeline, shot list, menu selections)?
- How do you handle timeline delays on the day-of?

**Communication:**

- What is your preferred communication method and typical response time?
- Who is our point of contact between now and the wedding?
- How many planning meetings or calls are included?

**Red flags that apply to every vendor type:**

- They pressure you to sign immediately or claim another couple is "about to book" the same date. Real vendors give you time to decide.
- They cannot provide references from recent clients.
- The contract is vague about deliverables, timelines, or what counts as extra charges.
- They get defensive when you ask detailed questions about pricing or backup plans.

## Photographer Questions

Photography is one of the few vendor purchases that outlasts the wedding day. The photos are what you keep. Everything else is temporary.

**Portfolio and style:**

- Can we see 2-3 full wedding galleries, not just highlight reels? (Highlight reels show 30 best shots. Full galleries show consistency.)
- Do you edit all photos or only selects? How many edited images can we expect?
- What is your turnaround time for delivering the final gallery?
- Do you have experience shooting at our venue or in similar lighting conditions?

**Day-of logistics:**

- How do you handle the family formal photo list? Do you work from our list or create your own?
- How long do you need for couple portraits? Where in the timeline do those fall?
- Will you bring backup equipment?
- Do you bring an assistant or second shooter? Is that included or extra?

**After the wedding:**

- How long do you keep our raw files?
- Do we own the rights to the images? Can we print them anywhere?
- What does an album cost, and is design included?
- Can we order prints through you, and how does pricing compare to third-party labs?

**Red flags specific to photographers:**

- They show you only heavily edited, stylized work but cannot show natural-light, candid shots.
- They do not have a backup camera body.
- They restrict where you can print your own photos.
- Their contract says they own the images and you only license them. (Common in the industry, but worth negotiating.)

## Caterer Questions

Food and drink is the largest single expense for most weddings, and the category where hidden fees add up fastest. Service charges, tax on top of service charges, overtime, staffing ratios, and minimum guarantees can push a quoted price up 30-40%.

**Menu and service:**

- Can we do a tasting before signing? Is there a fee?
- What is the per-person cost for our preferred service style (plated, buffet, stations)?
- Does the per-person price include appetizers during cocktail hour, or is that a separate charge?
- How do you handle dietary restrictions and allergies?
- What is your staff-to-guest ratio?

**Bar:**

- What bar package options do you offer (open bar, limited, beer/wine only)?
- Is bar pricing per-person or consumption-based?
- Can we supply our own alcohol and pay a corkage fee instead?
- Are bartender fees included or extra?
- Do you carry liability insurance for alcohol service?

**Hidden costs to ask about:**

- What is the service charge percentage, and is it a gratuity or a fee? (A "service charge" often goes to the company, not to the staff. Gratuity goes to staff. The difference matters for tipping.)
- Is tax applied before or after the service charge?
- What is the overtime rate if the event runs long?
- Are cake cutting fees, setup/breakdown fees, or linen fees separate?
- What is the minimum guest count guarantee, and what happens if we come in under it?

**Red flags specific to caterers:**

- The initial quote does not include service charge or tax. (A $150/person quote becomes $195+ once you add 20% service charge plus tax.)
- They cannot accommodate common dietary restrictions (vegetarian, gluten-free, dairy-free) without a surcharge.
- They require payment in full before the event with no holdback for service issues.

## DJ and Music Questions

A DJ or band controls the energy of your reception for 3-4 hours. The difference between a good one and a bad one is the difference between a full dance floor and guests checking their phones.

**Experience and style:**

- Can we hear a sample mix or see video of you performing at a wedding?
- How many weddings have you done in the past year?
- Do you take requests from guests? Can we provide a do-not-play list?
- How do you handle transitions between cocktail hour, dinner, and dancing?
- Will you serve as MC for introductions, toasts, and event flow?

**Equipment and logistics:**

- What equipment do you bring, and will it work at our venue?
- Have you worked at our venue before? (If not, will you visit to assess the space?)
- Do you bring backup equipment?
- What power and space requirements do you have?
- How early do you need to set up?

**Contract details:**

- What does your package include (hours, setup/breakdown, MC duties)?
- What is the overtime rate?
- Do you charge travel fees?
- Can we see the contract before booking?

**Red flags specific to DJs:**

- They cannot provide video or audio samples of their actual work at weddings.
- They plan to use the same playlist regardless of your preferences.
- They want to be the center of attention rather than supporting the event flow.
- They do not have backup equipment.

## Florist Questions

Flowers are one of the easiest categories to overspend on without realizing it, because pricing is per-arrangement and the total depends entirely on how many pieces you order.

**Design and pricing:**

- Can we see photos of arrangements you have done in a similar style/color palette to what we want?
- Do you charge a flat design fee, or is it built into the per-arrangement pricing?
- What flowers are in season for our wedding date, and how does that affect pricing?
- Can you work within a specific budget and tell us what is realistic for that number?
- What is your approach if a specific flower is unavailable close to the wedding date?

**Logistics:**

- Do you deliver and set up at the venue? Is that included?
- What time do you arrive for setup, and do you come back for breakdown?
- Do you provide vases and containers, or do we need to rent them?
- Can ceremony flowers be repurposed for the reception? (This saves money if planned in advance.)

**Red flags specific to florists:**

- They cannot tell you what specific flowers will cost and instead only quote "arrangements starting at..."
- They push expensive varieties when in-season alternatives would look similar.
- They do not visit the venue before quoting (size and logistics affect pricing significantly).

## Venue Questions

The venue contract is usually the first and largest one you sign. It also has the most potential for hidden fees because so many services may or may not be included.

**What is and is not included:**

- Does the rental fee include tables, chairs, and linens, or are those rented separately?
- Is there an in-house caterer, or can we bring our own? If in-house only, can we see their menu and pricing?
- Is alcohol service through the venue, or can we hire a separate bartender?
- Does the venue provide a coordinator, or do we need to hire one?
- What decorating restrictions exist (no candles, no confetti, no outside furniture)?

**Logistics and access:**

- What is the earliest we can access the space for setup?
- What time must the event end? What time must vendors be out?
- Is there a separate space for the ceremony, or just the reception?
- What is the rain plan for outdoor ceremonies?
- Where do the wedding party get ready? Is that space included?
- How much parking is available? Is there overflow parking or shuttle access?

**Insurance and liability:**

- Do we need to purchase event liability insurance?
- What is the damage deposit, and under what conditions is it forfeited?
- What happens if the venue has a problem (plumbing, power outage, construction next door)?

**Red flags specific to venues:**

- The contract has a "force majeure" clause that protects the venue but does not protect you.
- Overtime fees are not disclosed until after signing.
- They restrict you to a preferred vendor list with no outside options. (Preferred vendor lists sometimes mean vendors who pay the venue a referral fee, not vendors who are actually best.)
- They require an in-house caterer whose pricing is significantly above market rate.

## How to Use This List

Do not ask every question to every vendor. That would take hours and annoy people. Instead:

1. Start with the baseline questions for every vendor type.
2. Add the vendor-specific questions that matter most to your priorities.
3. Take notes during each meeting. After talking to 3 vendors in the same category, your notes become a comparison tool.
4. Flag any red flags that came up and weigh them against the vendor's strengths.

The point of these questions is not to interrogate vendors. It is to get clear, specific answers before you sign a contract that commits thousands of dollars. Vendors who are good at what they do will answer these questions easily. Vendors who dodge them are telling you something.

## A Note About Vendor Directories on Wedding Platforms

If you are finding vendors through The Knot, WeddingWire, or similar platforms, know that vendors can pay for prominent placement in those directories. It is advertising, not a neutral recommendation. March 2026 congressional and press coverage raised concerns about alleged deceptive business practices and paid-placement disclosure issues. That does not mean those vendors are bad. It means ranking can reflect advertising spend, not quality. Use the interview questions above to evaluate vendors on their actual answers, not their search ranking on a platform.
