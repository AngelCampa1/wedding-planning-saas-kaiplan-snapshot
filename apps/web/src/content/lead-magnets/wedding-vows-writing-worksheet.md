## Vow Structure

Personal wedding vows work best in three parts. This structure gives the vows a clear emotional arc without feeling formulaic.

**Part 1: The Opening (1-3 sentences)**
A specific, personal observation about this person or this relationship. This is not "I am so happy to be here" or "you are my best friend." It is a detail only you could say about them. A moment. A quality. Something that happened.

*Bad opening:* "You are my best friend and my soulmate."
*Better opening:* "You are the person who drove two hours in the rain when I called you, and you did not ask why. That is when I knew."

**Part 2: The Promises (3-5 specific promises)**
What you are actually committing to. Not "to love you in good times and bad" but the specific version of that for this specific person and this specific relationship. Promises that only apply to the two of you.

*Generic promise:* "I promise to support you in all your endeavors."
*Specific promise:* "I promise to keep showing up to your games even when I have no idea what is happening on the field."

**Part 3: The Closing (1-2 sentences)**
A brief, direct declaration of your commitment. This is the emotional landing. It should be short. The longer and more elaborate the closing, the less impact it has.

*Good closing:* "Today and every day, I choose you."
*Also good:* "I am yours."

## Word Count and Timing Guide

| Spoken Length | Target Word Count | Best For |
|--------------|------------------|---------|
| 45-60 seconds | 75-100 words | Couples who prefer brevity, short ceremony |
| 60-90 seconds | 100-150 words | Standard length, most ceremonies |
| 90-120 seconds | 150-200 words | Couples with a lot to say, comfortable public speakers |
| 2+ minutes | 200+ words | Only if genuinely justified; most vows benefit from editing |

Read your draft aloud and time it at the pace you will actually speak (nervous, slower than normal). This is the only accurate way to measure.

## Reflection Prompts

Work through these prompts alone, writing freely without editing. Aim for 3-5 sentences per prompt. You will not use everything you write. The goal is to surface specific memories and details you can draw from when drafting.

**Prompt 1: How we met**

Write down the facts of how you first encountered each other. What do you remember about that moment or period? What surprised you about them early on?

## Cliche Phrases to Cut or Replace

These phrases appear in almost every set of wedding vows and have lost their meaning through repetition. If you find them in your draft, replace them with the specific version from your own prompts.

| Cliche | Try Instead |
|--------|------------|
| "my best friend" | The specific thing a best friend does, that they do |
| "soulmate" | What it felt like when you realized this was the person |
| "through good times and bad" | The specific bad time you already survived together |
| "complete me" | What being with them has made possible for you specifically |
| "journey together" | Where you are actually going, concretely |
| "I never knew love like this" | The first time you felt it |
| "I choose you every day" | What choosing them looks like on a Tuesday morning |

## Final Checklist Before Wedding Day

- [ ] Read vows aloud (not silently) at least three times before the wedding
- [ ] Time yourself reading them aloud: \_\_\_\_\_ seconds
- [ ] Read them to at least one other person before the wedding
- [ ] Printed on a card or saved to phone with screen lock disabled
- [ ] Card is in a pocket/bag that is confirmed packed in getting-ready bag
- [ ] Confirmed length with partner so neither set dramatically overshadows the other
- [ ] Shared a copy with your officiant in case you need a prompt
