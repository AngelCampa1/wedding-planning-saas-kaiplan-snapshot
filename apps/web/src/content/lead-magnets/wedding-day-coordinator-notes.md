  - q: "Who should be the day-of coordinator if I did not hire a professional?"
    a: "Assign this role to someone who is NOT in the wedding party and does NOT have a family role in the ceremony. The maid of honor and parents are emotionally involved and cannot manage logistics while participating in the day. A trusted friend or family member who is detail-oriented and calm under pressure is the right person. Brief them with this template at least two weeks before the wedding."
  - q: "What should be in a day-of coordinator briefing?"
    a: "The coordinator needs: the complete vendor contact list with arrival windows, the full day timeline, venue access information (door codes, which entrance vendors use), location of the marriage license, location of the payment envelopes, and the name of the person at each vendor company who will be on-site. Walk them through the timeline and answer any questions in person, not over text."
  - q: "What are the most common day-of emergencies and how should I prepare for them?"
    a: "The five most common: a vendor running late (have a 15-minute buffer built into every transition), a family member not following the processional plan (brief all participants the night before at rehearsal), a wardrobe malfunction (emergency kit with safety pins, double-sided tape, and a sewing kit), unexpected weather (confirm the venue's rain plan in writing before the wedding), and a vendor no-show (have a backup contact for every vendor and confirm who to call if the primary contact is unreachable)."

## Troubleshooting Guide: Common Day-of Problems

### A Vendor Arrives Late

1. Call the vendor's cell phone immediately. Do not wait more than 10 minutes past their expected arrival.
2. If no answer, call the company's main line or emergency contact.
3. Assess whether the late arrival affects the timeline. If florist is late, can setup happen in parallel with other events?
4. Do not notify the couple unless the delay will materially affect the ceremony or reception.
5. If the vendor is more than 30 minutes late and unreachable, notify the venue coordinator and begin documenting with timestamps.

### Rain or Weather Change

1. Confirm the venue's backup plan before the day (this should be documented in the venue contract).
2. Communicate the plan to the wedding party and guests via a clear announcement: "The ceremony will move indoors to [location] due to weather."
3. Do not delay the ceremony more than 15-20 minutes hoping weather will clear. Make a decision and commit.
4. Coordinate with the venue coordinator on who communicates to guests.

### A Family Member Is Causing Problems

1. Do not involve the couple directly unless absolutely necessary.
2. Identify one calm family member trusted by the person causing the problem. Ask that person to manage the situation privately.
3. If the issue is about seating: quietly offer an alternative seat without drawing attention.
4. If the issue is about alcohol: alert the venue coordinator or bar staff to slow service for that person.
5. If the issue escalates to a point where the couple needs to be informed, do so briefly and privately between events.

### Wardrobe Malfunction

1. Emergency kit location: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
2. Safety pins, double-sided tape, and a sewing kit handle most dress or suit emergencies.
3. A stain on the dress: blot (do not rub) with cold water, then apply a stain remover pen and blot again. Do not wet the fabric excessively.
4. A broken heel: flat shoes or bare feet for the couple are better than limping through the reception.

### A Vendor No-Show

1. Attempt contact via every available number (cell, company line, emergency contact on this sheet).
2. For photographers: check whether the second shooter can cover lead duties.
3. For DJs or bands: a smartphone with a Bluetooth speaker and a curated playlist can cover cocktail hour. For full reception music, a last-minute DJ through a local agency may be available.
4. For catering no-shows: contact the venue coordinator immediately. This is a venue-level crisis, not something the coordinator handles alone.
5. Document everything with timestamps for the insurance or legal claim.

### Couple or Guest Medical Emergency

1. Call 911 first.
2. Inform the venue coordinator, who should know the fastest ambulance route to the property.
3. Assign one calm person to stay with the affected individual and one to manage the guest experience so the event continues if possible.
4. Inform the couple privately and let them decide whether to pause the event.
