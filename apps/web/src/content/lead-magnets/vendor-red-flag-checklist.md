## Before You Search: Platform Red Flags

The Knot and WeddingWire are useful starting directories. They are not unbiased quality rankings. Both platforms earn revenue from the vendors listed on them, which shapes what you see before you ever contact anyone.

A few things to know before you treat search results as endorsements:

Vendors can pay for higher placement. A free listing may appear below a newer vendor with premium paid visibility, even when review signals look stronger. "Best of Weddings" and "Couples' Choice" badges are tied to active marketplace participation, so treat them as platform signals rather than independent performance certifications.

Treat marketplace reviews as platform-bound, not fully independent. Vendors can dispute reviews, and some vendors have alleged that paid marketplace status can affect review visibility. Google Business reviews sit outside the wedding marketplace itself. If a vendor's ratings look uniform across every platform at 4.9-5.0, that uniformity is a signal worth noting.

Price ranges are withheld deliberately. Forcing you to submit an inquiry before seeing any pricing creates a sunk cost before you know whether a vendor fits your budget. Vendors who won't give ballpark ranges in first contact are protecting a pitch, not your time.

**Before contacting any vendor:**

- [ ] **Check Google Reviews independently** — search the business name plus "reviews" and read the critical ones first
- [ ] **Note if everyone in a category has identical 4.9-5.0 stars** — homogenized ratings across a directory reflect curation, not consistent quality
- [ ] **Treat featured placement as paid advertising** — paid placement can affect visibility, so do not treat listing order as a reputation ranking
- [ ] **Search Reddit r/weddingplanning for the vendor name** — real couples post experiences that platforms cannot suppress
- [ ] **Search "[vendor name] + complaints" and "[vendor name] + BBB"** before requesting a quote

## First Contact: Communication Red Flags

How a vendor handles your first inquiry tells you how they'll handle everything else. These are the patterns worth noting before you invest time in a consultation.

- [ ] **Refuses to share any pricing range in first contact** — vendors with nothing to hide give ballpark numbers upfront; those who won't are managing your expectations before revealing the price
- [ ] **Uses availability scarcity language** ("we only have 2 dates left for your month") on a cold inquiry — this is a sales tactic, not a scheduling reality
- [ ] **Sends a contract before an itemized quote** — you should have a line-item breakdown before you see a signature page
- [ ] **Gives a single all-in number without a breakdown** — any vendor who resists itemization is hiding a cost somewhere in the total
- [ ] **Requires more than 50% deposit immediately** — a large upfront deposit before you've read the contract in full is a pressure tactic
- [ ] **Does not volunteer references** — ask for contact information from two clients from the past 12 months, not from a testimonials page they control

## The Venue Contract: Where Budgets Collapse

The food and beverage minimum is the most common source of catastrophic budget overrun in wedding planning. It is almost never disclosed in venue marketing, and some venues reveal it only after you've toured the space and mentally committed to the location.

The minimum is a real number you are contractually obligated to meet. If your guest count comes in low or your menu choices don't add up to the threshold, you pay the difference anyway.

**Before scheduling any venue tour:**

- [ ] **Ask directly in your first message: "Do you have a food and beverage minimum?"** — get the number before you visit, not after you fall in love with the space
- [ ] **Get the F&B minimum in writing before your site visit** — some venues disclose $40,000-75,000 minimums only after you've toured
- [ ] **Ask whether outside caterers are permitted** — if not, ask for the outside vendor buyout fee in writing
- [ ] **Request a sample invoice from a recent event at your expected guest count** — add the line items yourself to see the real total
- [ ] **Read the overtime clause** — know the per-hour charge before you sign, not after the reception runs long

**General venue contract checks:**

- [ ] **Confirm whether setup and breakdown time is included** in the rental window or counted against it
- [ ] **Ask what happens if the venue's staff coordinator is unavailable** on your date
- [ ] **Check whether the venue's preferred vendor list is required or just recommended** — required vendor lists limit your options and are worth knowing early

## The Vendor Contract: General Checks

These apply to every vendor you sign with, not just venues.

- [ ] **Look for an automatic renewal clause** — some vendor contracts include renewal language worth removing before you sign
- [ ] **Read the force majeure clause specifically** — confirm it covers vendor illness or staffing failure, not just acts of nature
- [ ] **Ask for the substitution policy in writing** — what happens if the photographer or DJ you contracted isn't available on your date?
- [ ] **Confirm gratuity is not pre-included** — so you know what's expected on top of the contract amount
- [ ] **Check whether vendor meals are required** — most catering contracts include language requiring you to feed vendors on-site; this adds $25-50 per vendor to your catering bill
- [ ] **Verify cancellation and refund terms** — understand what you lose if you cancel 6, 3, and 1 month out

## After Signing: Ongoing Verification

Signing a contract is not the end of the process. Vendors change staff, ownership, and availability. Confirming details at regular intervals protects you from surprises on the day.

- [ ] **Follow up 90 days before your date** with every vendor to confirm all contracted details are still accurate
- [ ] **Request a revised quote if you've made any changes** since signing — adds and adjustments compound
- [ ] **Verify liability insurance** if your venue requires vendors to carry it
- [ ] **Confirm day-of contact information** 2 weeks out — who specifically will be there, and what number reaches them directly
- [ ] **Reread overtime terms** the week before — know every vendor's contracted end time and what the per-hour charge is if you go over
