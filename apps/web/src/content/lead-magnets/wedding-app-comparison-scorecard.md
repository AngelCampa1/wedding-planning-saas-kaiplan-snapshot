## How to Use This Scorecard

Rate each wedding planning app on the six categories below. Score each category from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent) using the specific criteria listed. Total the scores for a side-by-side comparison.

This is not about which app has the most features. It is about which app helps you make better decisions about your wedding. An app with 50 features that steers you toward paid vendor placements is worse than an app with 10 features that shows you accurate numbers.

**The six categories:**

1. Budget Tracking Quality (weight: high)
2. Guest List Management (weight: medium)
3. Vendor Directory Neutrality (weight: high)
4. Seating and Logistics (weight: medium)
5. Pricing Model and Value (weight: high)
6. Data Ownership and Portability (weight: medium)

Why these six and not others? Because these are the categories where apps differ the most, and where the differences actually affect your wedding outcomes. Every app has a photo gallery feature. Not every app gives you honest budget data.

## Category 1: Budget Tracking Quality

This is the single most important feature in a wedding planning tool. If the budget tracker does not work well, you need a separate spreadsheet, and the app has failed its primary job.

**Score 5 if the app:**

- Tracks estimated cost, quoted cost, and final paid cost separately for each vendor
- Includes all standard wedding categories pre-loaded (venue, catering, photography, etc.) plus the commonly missed ones (gratuities, transportation, insurance, overtime fees)
- Shows running totals and remaining budget in real time
- Tracks deposits paid, balances due, and payment deadlines
- Allows custom categories for non-standard expenses

**Score 3 if the app:**

- Has a basic budget feature but only tracks one number per vendor (estimated or paid, not both)
- Includes major categories but misses smaller ones
- Does not track payment schedules or deposit dates
- Works for overall budget monitoring but does not help with cash flow timing

**Score 1 if the app:**

- Has a "budget" section that is really just a list with no calculations
- Does not separate estimates from actuals
- Omits critical categories (gratuities, overtime, service charges)
- You will need a separate spreadsheet within a week of using it

**Questions to test this category:**

- Can you enter a vendor quote and then update it with the actual final cost?
- Does the app remind you when a payment is due?
- Does it show how much of your total budget is committed vs. still available?
- Can you add custom line items that are not in the pre-loaded categories?

## Category 2: Guest List Management

Guest list management sounds simple until you have 150 invitations to track, each with potentially 2-3 guests, plus dietary restrictions, plus table assignments, plus mailing addresses.

**Score 5 if the app:**

- Handles individual guests and households/groups (invite one household, track RSVPs per person)
- Tracks RSVP status (invited, accepted, declined, no response) per guest
- Stores dietary restrictions and meal preferences per person
- Manages plus-ones and children separately
- Integrates with or replaces your RSVP system (so you are not double-entering data from a separate RSVP website)
- Exports guest data (names, addresses, dietary info) for vendor sharing

**Score 3 if the app:**

- Basic guest tracking (name, RSVP status)
- Does not handle household grouping well
- Limited dietary/meal tracking
- No RSVP integration (you use a separate tool and manually update)
- Export is limited or clunky

**Score 1 if the app:**

- Guest list is essentially a contact list with no wedding-specific fields
- No RSVP tracking
- No dietary management
- You will end up using a spreadsheet

## Category 3: Vendor Directory Neutrality

This is the category most wedding apps fail on, because advertising is how many of them make money. An app that makes revenue from vendor placements has a financial incentive to show you the vendors who pay the most, not the vendors who are best for your wedding.

**Score 5 if the app:**

- Has no vendor advertising or paid placements in any search results or recommendations
- If it has a vendor directory, results are not ranked by advertising spend
- The app makes money from subscriptions or one-time fees, not from vendor referral commissions
- Any vendor recommendations are clearly labeled as curated by editorial staff, not sold to the highest bidder

**Score 3 if the app:**

- Has a vendor directory with some paid placements, but they are clearly labeled as "sponsored" or "featured"
- The app has a mixed revenue model (some subscription, some vendor advertising)
- You can use the planning tools without engaging with the vendor directory

**Score 1 if the app:**

- The vendor directory is the primary product, and planning tools are secondary features designed to keep you on the platform
- Paid vendor placements are not clearly labeled
- Vendor "recommendations" are based on who pays for positioning
- The app is free because the vendors are the real customers, not you

**Why this matters:** When a platform makes money from vendor advertising, every "recommendation" is suspect. The photographer at the top of the search results may be there because they pay $500/month for that spot, not because they are the best fit for your wedding. The Knot and WeddingWire operate on this model. That is not a secret, but many couples do not realize it until after they have booked.

## Category 4: Seating and Logistics

Seating charts are the feature that sounds optional until you try to seat 120 people at 15 tables while keeping your divorced parents at separate tables, your college friends near each other, and your partner's work colleagues somewhere they will not feel awkward.

**Score 5 if the app:**

- Visual drag-and-drop seating chart with multiple table shapes (round, rectangular, head table)
- Automatically warns about conflicts (guests flagged as "keep apart")
- Links to guest list (meal choices appear on the seating view)
- Supports multiple layout options for different venue configurations
- Generates a printable seating chart and place card list

**Score 3 if the app:**

- Has a seating chart feature but it is basic (assign guests to table numbers, no visual layout)
- No conflict flagging
- Does not pull meal preferences into the seating view
- Limited export options

**Score 1 if the app:**

- No seating chart feature
- You will need a separate tool or a physical board with Post-it notes

## Category 5: Pricing Model and Value

How a wedding app charges you tells you something about whose interests it prioritizes. Free apps funded by vendor advertising serve the vendors. Subscription apps serve the subscribers. One-time purchase apps serve the buyer once and then have no ongoing relationship.

**Score 5 if the app:**

- Clear, upfront pricing with no hidden fees
- Pricing is reasonable relative to the value (a wedding planning app should not cost more per month than a streaming service unless it does something exceptional)
- No feature gating that cripples the free tier to force upgrades (basic budget and guest list should work without paying)
- Does not require you to share data with advertisers in exchange for free access
- Offers a lifetime purchase option if you prefer not to subscribe

**Score 3 if the app:**

- Free tier exists but is heavily limited (feature-gated or ad-supported)
- Paid tier is reasonably priced but locks basic features (budget tracking, guest list) behind the paywall
- No lifetime option (subscription only)

**Score 1 if the app:**

- "Free" but funded entirely by vendor advertising and data sharing
- Paid tiers are expensive relative to what you get
- Features you need are spread across multiple pricing tiers to force the highest tier
- The app sells your contact information to vendors (results in spam from photographers, DJs, caterers you never contacted)

## Category 6: Data Ownership and Portability

This matters more than people think. Your wedding data includes your guest list with addresses, your budget with vendor contracts and payment history, and your seating arrangements. If the app shuts down, gets acquired, or you decide to switch tools, what happens to that data?

**Score 5 if the app:**

- You can export all your data (guest list, budget, vendor info) in standard formats (CSV, PDF)
- Your data is yours. The terms of service do not grant the app a license to use your data for marketing
- You can delete your account and your data is actually deleted
- The app does not require you to create vendor accounts on partner platforms to access features

**Score 3 if the app:**

- Partial export available (guest list exports but budget does not, or only PDF not CSV)
- Terms of service are vague about data usage
- Account deletion is possible but data retention policy is unclear

**Score 1 if the app:**

- No export. Your data is locked in the app.
- The terms of service grant broad rights to use your data
- You cannot delete your account, or the app retains your data indefinitely after deletion
- Your guest email addresses end up on vendor marketing lists

## A Shortcut for Fast Evaluation

If you do not want to score all six categories in depth, ask these three questions about any wedding planning app. They cover the most critical ground:

1. **How does the app make money?** If the answer is vendor advertising, your "recommendations" are ads. If the answer is a subscription or one-time fee, the app is working for you.
2. **Can the budget tracker show me estimated vs. actual cost per vendor?** If no, you will need a separate spreadsheet within a month.
3. **Can I export my guest list and budget data as a CSV?** If no, you are locked in.
