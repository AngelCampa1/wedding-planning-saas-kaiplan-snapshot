## Table Layout Options

Choose your table configuration before you start assigning guests. Mixing table sizes is fine, but confirm the final layout with your venue.

### Round Tables

Round tables are the most common for wedding receptions. They encourage conversation because everyone can see everyone else at the table.

| Table Size | Comfortable Seating | Maximum Seating |
|------------|--------------------|-----------------| 
| 48-inch round | 6 guests | 8 guests |
| 60-inch round | 8 guests | 10 guests |
| 72-inch round | 10 guests | 12 guests |

**Best for:** Venues with good sightlines, receptions where guests know each other within groups.

### Rectangular (Banquet) Tables

Rectangular tables work well for smaller weddings and family-style meals. Conversation tends to be limited to the person next to you rather than the whole table.

| Table Size | Comfortable Seating | Maximum Seating |
|------------|--------------------|-----------------| 
| 6-foot rectangle | 6 guests | 8 guests |
| 8-foot rectangle | 8 guests | 10 guests |

**Best for:** Smaller weddings, farm tables for a more casual aesthetic, long communal seating arrangements.

## Guest Grouping Strategy

The fastest way to build a seating chart is to start with clusters of people who already know each other, then decide how to mix them.

### Step 1: Sort Guests into Natural Groups

Write group names and the guests in each group. Use a separate sheet or sticky notes if you have a large guest list.

| Mutual friends | | |
| Other: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ | | |
| Other: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ | | |

### Step 2: Decide Your Mixing Approach

**Pure clusters:** Seat groups entirely at their own tables. Easiest logistics, but the wedding can feel like separate parties.

**Light mixing:** Seat each group primarily together, but add 1-2 guests from another compatible group to each table. This works well for couples whose friend groups have not fully met yet.

**Full mixing:** Seat guests entirely by compatibility rather than group. Works best when the couple knows most guests well and can predict personality matches.

**Recommendation:** Pure clusters for family tables. Light mixing for friend tables.

## Common Seating Questions

**What do I do with plus-ones I have never met?**
Seat them next to their partner and include them in a table group where their partner knows people. Do not seat unknown plus-ones at a table where they will be the only person their partner does not know.

**What if two groups genuinely dislike each other?**
Separate tables, and if the tension is severe, put other guests between them as a buffer. Do not seat them where they will have to walk past each other repeatedly (near the bar, dance floor entrance, etc.).

**Should children have their own table?**
Children 10 and older can usually sit at an adult table without issue. Children 5-9 benefit from sitting near parents. A kids' table works well for weddings with 8+ children, ideally positioned near a parent table. Provide activities (crayons, small games) and confirm the caterer has a children's menu or can accommodate simpler options.

**What if I am still waiting on RSVPs?**
Build the seating chart with confirmed guests first, then hold 2-3 seats at strategic tables as "pending" slots. When late RSVPs come in, fill the closest compatible table first. Do not save prime seats for people who cannot be bothered to RSVP on time.
