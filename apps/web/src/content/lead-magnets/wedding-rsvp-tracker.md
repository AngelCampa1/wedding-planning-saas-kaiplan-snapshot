## Guest List Tracking Template

Use one row per adult guest (not per household). Children under a specific age who share a parent's meal do not need their own row, but children with their own seat at the table should be tracked separately.

**How to set up:** Build this in a spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel, or similar) with one row per person. The columns below represent every field you need from invitation through final headcount submission.

| Column | Purpose | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------|
| Guest ID # | Matches the number on the back of physical RSVP card | Prevents unidentified returns |
| Last Name | Sort by this for seating chart work | |
| First Name | | |
| Household / Party | Groups guests who arrive together | Used for escort card creation |
| Invitation Sent Date | Confirms when mail was sent | |
| RSVP Status | Attending / Not Attending / Pending | Update as responses come in |
| RSVP Received Date | Tracks who responded before vs. after deadline | |
| Attending Headcount | Number of seats needed for this guest/party | |
| Meal Choice | If caterer requires it: Chicken / Beef / Veg / etc. | |
| Dietary Restrictions | Allergies and preferences, distinct from meal choice | Critical for caterer submission |
| Plus-One Name | If different from the invited name | |
| Hotel Block | Yes / No / Booked | Helps with room block accuracy |
| Table Assignment | Fill in when seating chart is finalized | |
| Thank You Sent | Yes / No | Track post-wedding |
| Notes | Accessibility needs, late arrivals, anything special | |

## RSVP Follow-Up Timeline and Strategy

### The Follow-Up Schedule

| Timing | Action |
|--------|--------|
| 2 weeks before RSVP deadline | Send a reminder to guests who have not responded (email or text, brief and friendly) |
| RSVP deadline | Note who has and has not responded |
| 1 day after deadline | Begin phone/text follow-up with non-responders |
| 5 days after deadline | Final attempts to reach non-responders |
| 7 days after deadline | Make a decision for unresponsive guests (see decision framework below) |

### How to Follow Up Without Creating Drama

**By text (most effective for younger guests):**
> "Hey [name], just following up on the wedding invite. The RSVP deadline passed and we haven't heard from you. Are you planning to come? We need a final count for the caterer. No worries either way."

**By phone (best for older family members):**
Keep it brief and warm. The goal is information, not a conversation. "Hi [name], I'm calling to see if you got a chance to RSVP for the wedding. We're finalizing numbers for the caterer and wanted to make sure we have you in the count."

**What not to do:** Do not send mass follow-up emails (too easy to ignore), do not guilt-trip (people genuinely forget and shame does not help), and do not ask the couple to do all the follow-up calls themselves.

### Decision Framework for Unreachable Guests

If you have genuinely tried 3 times via different channels and cannot reach a guest within one week of the deadline:

- **Close family or friend:** One final attempt by a family member who knows them better. If still no response, mark as not attending.
- **Acquaintance or distant relation:** Mark as not attending after 3 attempts. You cannot hold catering numbers for someone who will not respond.
- **Already told you in person they are coming:** Mark as attending. Assume they forgot to return the card.

### Handling Late RSVPs After Submission

Some guests will RSVP after you have already submitted your headcount to the caterer. Here is how to handle it:

- **Late "Yes" (wants to attend but missed deadline):** Check with caterer whether additions are possible, and what the per-person charge is. Many caterers allow additions up to 5-7 days before the event for a fee. Do not feel obligated to accommodate late acceptances if the caterer cannot.
- **Late "No" (confirms they cannot attend after saying yes):** Notify the caterer as soon as possible. Some caterers will adjust; others are locked in at the guaranteed minimum. Adjust the seating chart.
- **Day-of no-shows:** Common. Budget for 5-10% of confirmed guests not actually appearing. Do not rearrange seating on the day; just leave the seat empty.
