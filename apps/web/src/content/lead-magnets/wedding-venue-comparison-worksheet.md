  - q: "What is the most commonly overlooked clause in a venue contract?"
    a: "The vendor exclusivity clause. Many venues require you to use their preferred caterer, bartending service, or both. This locks you into their pricing with no competitive bids.   - q: "When is the deposit refundable if I have to cancel?"
    a: "Almost never in full, and often not at all. Most venue contracts treat the deposit as liquidated damages if you cancel within 12 months of the event. ## Scoring Rubric

Use this when filling out the 1-5 score for each criterion.

**5 - Exceeds expectations.** The venue handles this better than any other option you have seen. This is a genuine differentiator.

**4 - Solid.** Above average. This criterion does not create any problems or constraints for your wedding.

**3 - Acceptable.** The venue meets minimum standards but nothing stands out. You would not choose this venue because of this criterion, but you would not reject it either.

**2 - Problematic.** This criterion creates a real constraint or added cost. You can work around it, but it requires concessions (more budget, fewer options, etc.).

**1 - Dealbreaker candidate.** This criterion would make the venue a poor fit for your specific wedding. Proceed only if you are willing to fully accept this limitation.

### Weighted Scoring Option

If some criteria matter more to your wedding than others, apply a weight multiplier:

- **3x (critical):** Criteria that directly determine whether your wedding vision is achievable. Common examples: catering policy (if you have a specific caterer in mind), capacity (if you have a fixed guest list), rain backup (if you are planning an outdoor ceremony).
- **2x (important):** Criteria that significantly affect budget or logistics. Common examples: alcohol policy, overtime fees, outside vendor policy.
- **1x (standard):** Everything else.

Multiply each score by its weight, then total. Divide by the maximum possible score to get a percentage.

## Questions to Ask Before You Leave

After your tour, ask these before you walk out the door. Do not assume the answers are obvious.

**On exclusivity:**
- Are there other events happening the same day? Can guests from another event access our area?
- If shared, what time does the prior event end and cleanup begin?

**On food and drink:**
- If we use your in-house catering, do prices include service charge and tax, or is that added on top?
- What happens if we go over the beverage minimum estimate?
- Can guests bring wine from a specific vineyard (common for vineyard weddings)?

**On logistics:**
- What is the earliest time our vendors can arrive to set up?
- Who is the point of contact on the day of our wedding?
- Where do vendors load in? Is there an elevator?

**On contracts:**
- What does your force majeure clause cover? (Fire, flood, public health emergencies?)
- Has the venue ever been sold or changed management? What happens to contracts if it changes hands?
- What insurance does the venue carry, and what does the couple need to provide?
