## Who Aisle Planner Is Actually Built For

Aisle Planner was designed for professional wedding planners managing multiple client events. The budget tools, timeline management, and vendor tracking reflect what a professional planner needs to do their job: detailed financial reporting, complex timeline dependencies, and client-ready documentation.

This is not a criticism. It is the reason Aisle Planner's budget tools are actually good. When you build software for someone who tracks money professionally, the tracking tools end up capable.

The side effect for couples: you are using professional tools for a single-event use case, and many of the features are irrelevant. The learning curve is steeper than consumer-facing tools because the interface was not designed with one-time users in mind.

## What The Knot Is Actually Built For

The Knot was designed to connect couples with vendors. The vendor directory is its core product; the planning tools are features added to keep couples engaged on the platform so they remain reachable by vendors.

The budget tool is adequate for early planning - getting a rough sense of how to allocate a total budget across categories. It is not a real financial management tool. The Knot's business does not require one.

## The Budget Tracking Comparison

Aisle Planner: enter your budget, add vendors with quoted prices, log deposits as you pay them, set payment schedules, track remaining balances per vendor. The tool maintains a running picture of your actual financial position.

The Knot: enter your total budget, see suggested category allocations, adjust the allocations, enter planned amounts. No actual payment tracking. No deposit logging. No real remaining balance.

These are fundamentally different tools. Aisle Planner's budget section does what a spreadsheet does. The Knot's budget section is a planning template.

## The Cost Comparison Over a Typical Engagement

For a 12-month engagement: Aisle Planner runs $600-$2,760 in subscription fees. The Knot is free. Kaiplan is $100 for lifetime access  (or $20/month if you prefer).

For couples who need real budget tracking, the comparison between Aisle Planner and Kaiplan is more relevant than the comparison with The Knot. Both have real ledger tools; the questions are interface complexity and cost.

Aisle Planner has more features. Kaiplan has a simpler interface built specifically for couples rather than professional planners. Over a typical engagement, Kaiplan's lifetime option costs significantly less than Aisle Planner's subscription.
