## Be Honest About Spreadsheets

Spreadsheets are genuinely good. Google Sheets is free, flexible, and most people already know how to use it. For some couples, a spreadsheet is the right answer — and we'd rather say that clearly than pretend Kaiplan is the only reasonable option.

A spreadsheet handles a wedding budget well if you're willing to build it. It handles a guest list well. It can track vendor contacts. What it can't do gracefully is all four things together, in a way that stays synchronized as details change over 14 months of planning.

## Where Spreadsheets Work

Small weddings. Under 75 guests, maybe 4-6 vendors, one venue. At that scale, you can keep everything in your head and a spreadsheet, and the integration problem (guest count in tab A doesn't auto-update seating in tab B) stays manageable because you're updating things infrequently.

Couples who already have a system. If you're 6 months into planning with a spreadsheet that works, switching now costs time and risks losing data. The value case for switching is lower when you're already past the setup phase.

People who enjoy building systems. Some people genuinely like building spreadsheets. If the process of creating formulas and organizing data is satisfying rather than tedious, a spreadsheet gives you more control than any purpose-built app.

## Where Spreadsheets Break Down

Seating charts. Seating a wedding in a spreadsheet means cells, color-coding, and hours of manual rearranging every time someone changes their RSVP. It works, technically. It's also miserable, especially with more than 60 people.

Integration across systems. Your guest count lives in your guest tab. Your per-person catering cost lives in your budget tab. Your seating chart lives in its own tab. When a family of four declines two weeks before the wedding, you update three places. And then check for the fourth place you forgot. Spreadsheets don't enforce this linkage; you do, manually, every time.

Formula errors. Spreadsheet errors are silent. A SUM formula that missed a row, a reference to a deleted cell, a VLOOKUP that returns blank instead of an error — these are real and common. The budget that seems balanced may have a broken formula you haven't noticed. Purpose-built tools validate inputs and catch these problems.

## What Kaiplan Actually Adds

The short version: structure you don't have to build, and integration you'd have to manually maintain.

Budget tracking in Kaiplan records actual contracts — not category estimates, but the real number from the real contract. It tracks deposits paid and outstanding balances automatically. When your caterer invoice comes in, you enter it once, and the running total updates.

Vendor management keeps contacts, contract amounts, and payment schedules in one view. Not a list in a spreadsheet tab — a structured record with the fields vendors actually require.

Seating charts in a purpose-built tool are drag-and-drop instead of cell-by-cell rearranging.

The guest list, vendor list, budget, and seating chart share data. Guest count affects the budget; RSVP responses update the seating chart. That integration is the hard part to replicate in a spreadsheet.

## The Honest Cost-Benefit

Kaiplan Starter is $20/month. Over 14 months, that's $280. The Lifetime plan is $100 one-time — and for most couples, that's the right answer. $100 to avoid building a wedding budget system from scratch is a reasonable trade.

If $100 is genuinely a stretch, use Google Sheets with a good template and invest 3-4 hours in the setup. It'll work. Just understand what you're signing up for.
