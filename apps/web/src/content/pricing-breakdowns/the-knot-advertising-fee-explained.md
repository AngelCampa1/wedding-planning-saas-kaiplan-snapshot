## How The Knot Makes Money

The Knot's business model is vendor advertising. Couples use the platform for free. Vendors — photographers, venues, florists, caterers — pay monthly fees to appear in the directory, with higher fees buying better placement.

This model built The Knot into the largest US wedding vendor directory. It also creates a structural tension: the platform's revenue depends on vendor advertising, and the vendor-facing product (directory placement, lead generation) competes for resources with the couple-facing product (planning tools, budget tracker, website builder).

Understanding that tension helps couples use The Knot more effectively.

## What Vendors Actually Pay

The Knot's vendor advertising packages vary significantly by market and category. In major markets like New York, Los Angeles, and Chicago, premium categories like venues and photographers command higher rates — $800-$1,500/month for featured placement isn't unusual for competitive urban markets. In smaller markets, rates are lower.

Vendors make this investment to appear at the top of search results and receive qualified leads. The math works when the cost per acquired client is lower than the advertising spend. For popular categories in competitive markets, that math works. For vendors in niche categories or smaller markets, the economics are harder, which is why smaller-market vendor coverage on The Knot can be thin.

## The FTC Investigation Context

In 2021, the FTC opened an investigation into XO Group — The Knot's parent company — related to advertising disclosures in its vendor marketplace. The core question was whether couples were sufficiently informed that the recommendations and vendor placements they saw were advertising-supported rather than neutral quality rankings.

The Knot is not unique in this model — most large vendor directories operate this way. The investigation reflects broader scrutiny of how advertising relationships are disclosed in platform recommendations. Couples don't always realize that "top rated" or "featured" designations in a vendor marketplace reflect advertising spend alongside genuine quality.

## What This Means for Couples

The practical impact is straightforward: treat The Knot's vendor search as a starting directory, not a quality ranking.

Good use of The Knot's directory: use it to find vendors in your category and market, gather initial pricing information, and see who serves your area. Many excellent vendors appear in The Knot.

Less good use: assume that a vendor's position in search results reflects their quality relative to unlisted or lower-placed vendors. Some of the best vendors in a market don't advertise on The Knot at all because the economics don't work for their business model.

For vendor discovery, combining The Knot with Google, direct referrals, and independent review platforms gives a more complete picture than The Knot alone.

## Planning Tools Separate from Vendor Directory

The Knot's planning tools — checklist, budget tracker, guest list, wedding website — are available regardless of which vendors you discover through the platform. You don't need to use The Knot's vendor directory to use its planning tools.

The planning tool quality is comparable to other free platforms: functional for early-stage planning, limited for execution-phase budget and vendor management.
