## The Same Company, Two Brands

Most couples don't know that WeddingWire and The Knot are owned by the same company. They encounter both in searches, see different designs and interfaces, and assume they're comparing two independent platforms.

They're not. WeddingPro (formerly XO Group) owns both. The vendor advertising model is the same on both platforms. The businesses compete for brand awareness with couples while selling the same vendors the ability to advertise on both networks simultaneously.

This matters for how you use both platforms. Cross-referencing WeddingWire and The Knot results is not independent validation — it's comparing two presentations of the same underlying vendor pool.

## How Vendor Lead Fees Work

WeddingWire charges vendors in two ways: monthly advertising fees for directory listings and placement, and sometimes per-lead fees depending on the advertising package. The distinction matters for vendors, not directly for couples.

Monthly advertising fees guarantee directory presence and a level of search visibility. Per-lead fees charge vendors when a couple contacts them through the platform. These models create incentives for the platform to maximize lead volume — which isn't always aligned with generating the most relevant leads for either couples or vendors.

For vendors evaluating advertising ROI, the lead quality from WeddingWire varies significantly by category and market. For couples, the vendor's advertising costs eventually factor into their pricing.

## The Planning Tool Picture

WeddingWire includes planning tools similar to The Knot: checklist, budget estimator, guest list, and a wedding website builder. The tools are functional for early-stage planning and are not the platform's core product investment.

The same limitations that apply to The Knot's planning tools apply here. Budget estimation doesn't track actual payments. The planning features are retention mechanisms designed to keep couples engaged with the vendor directory, not purpose-built planning workflows.

For couples who want a planning tool that isn't also a vendor marketplace, the distinction is worth making before committing to either platform for 18 months of planning.
