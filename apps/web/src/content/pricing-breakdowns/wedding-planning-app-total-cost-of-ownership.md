## How Total Cost of Ownership Works for Wedding Software

Most software buying decisions get evaluated on sticker price. A $0 app looks cheaper than a $35/month app - until you account for what the $0 app actually costs when you add up all the fees, workarounds, and switching costs over an 18-month engagement.

We built Kaiplan partly because the TCO math on the major free platforms surprised us when we ran it honestly. This post shows the calculation so you can do it for your own situation.

The methodology: take a representative couple with a 15-month engagement, 120 guests, a $28,000 budget, and a mixed cash-and-gift registry. Then run the actual costs of four common software paths from engagement to one week after the wedding.

## Path 1: The DIY Stack (Google Sheets + Notion)

The DIY stack is genuinely free in software costs. The tools are:

- Google Sheets for budget tracking (a well-structured template handles actuals vs. estimates, vendor payment schedules, and running balance)
- Notion for vendor notes, checklists, and task management
- Carrd or Google Sites for a wedding website and RSVP collection

**Where costs appear:**

A custom domain for the wedding website costs $10-$12 per year at Namecheap, Cloudflare Registrar, or similar. You register it once; it covers the engagement period.

For RSVP collection, Google Forms is free and works, but the UX is basic. You can build something more polished inside Carrd for $19/year (Starter plan), which gets you a custom domain connection and better design control.

**Total platform cost over 15 months:** $10-$31 depending on whether you use Carrd or a free website option.

**Trade-offs:** The DIY stack requires manual configuration. A wedding budget spreadsheet built from scratch (or from a free template) works, but it does not have integrated vendor payment reminders, a seating chart tool, or automated RSVP reporting. Time cost is higher. If you are comfortable with spreadsheets, the tool works. If you are not, you will hit friction points that make the free cost feel expensive.

**Switching cost out:** Zero. Your data lives in your Google account and Notion workspace. You can export or migrate anything any time.

## Path 2: Freemium (The Knot + Zola)

The most common pattern for couples not paying for wedding software: The Knot for vendor discovery and checklists, Zola for registry and wedding website.

**Direct costs:**

The Knot's free tier includes a wedding website on a theknot.com subdomain. A custom domain upgrade through The Knot's system costs $15-$20 per year. If you want to skip the subdomain, budget $15-$20.

Zola's cash registry charges a 2.5% withdrawal fee. For a typical cash fund component, say $3,000 in cash gifts collected, you lose $75 in withdrawal fees. A $5,000 cash component costs $125.

Joy's free tier covers 75 guests. If your final headcount is 120, you need Joy Premium. At $9/month, that is $135 over 15 months. At $19/month for the higher tier, it is $285.

**Total direct platform costs over 15 months:** $15-$505 depending on cash registry size, guest count, and domain preferences.

**The embedded costs you do not see on the platform:**

The Knot and WeddingWire (both owned by The Knot Worldwide) generate revenue by selling advertising to vendors. Vendors in competitive markets spend $1,000-$30,000 per year on platform advertising. That cost is part of their overhead, which factors into the prices they quote.

You do not pay The Knot directly for this. But you are paying for it through the vendor economy. This is genuinely hard to quantify per-couple - the effect is diffuse. The better way to think about it: the vendor directory you are using is an advertising marketplace, and the best-placed vendors are not there because they won a quality competition.

**Switching cost out:** Moderate. Zola does not offer a free-tier guest list CSV export in all cases. The Knot's free tier does not have a budget export. Moving data out requires manual work - typically 3-6 hours for a 120-guest wedding.

## Path 3: Paid Subscription (Kaiplan Pro or similar)

A purpose-built paid wedding planning app charges directly. The cost is visible and predictable. For Kaiplan Pro at $35/month:

- 12 months of planning: $420
- 15 months: $525
- 18 months: $630

The Starter plan at $20/month:

- 12 months: $240
- 15 months: $300
- 18 months: $360

**What you do not pay on top:**

A paid planning app that earns from subscriptions has no vendor marketplace. The tool does not generate revenue from vendor advertising, so vendor search (if it exists) is not a curated ad placement. Registry, stationery, and invitation ordering are not integrated revenue lines.

Data export is included at any tier. If you decide to switch or cancel, you can pull your guest list, budget data, and vendor records in a standard format.

**Total platform cost over 15 months:** $300-$525 in direct subscription fees, depending on tier. No domain fees (wedding website included). No vendor ad cost embedded in the product decision loop.

**Trade-off vs. free:** The monthly cost is real and visible. For a $28,000 wedding, 15 months of Kaiplan Pro at $35/month is less than 2% of total wedding spend. Whether that is worth it depends on how much time you lose to the limitations of free tools, and how much the embedded costs of free platforms actually cost you.

## Path 4: Lifetime Deal (Kaiplan Lifetime)

Kaiplan's lifetime plan is $100 paid once. That is the total platform cost for the duration of your engagement, regardless of length.

- 3-month engagement: $100 total (vs. $105 for 3 months of Pro)
- 12-month engagement: $100 total (vs. $420 for 12 months of Pro)
- 18-month engagement: $100 total (vs. $630 for 18 months of Pro)

For any engagement over three months, the lifetime deal costs less than a monthly Pro subscription. For the average 15-month engagement, it is $425 less expensive than the subscription.

The lifetime plan is the straightforward answer to the TCO question for most couples: you pay once and use the tool for as long as you need it.

## The Registry Fee Math in Detail

Cash registries are popular because they let guests contribute to a honeymoon, a home down payment, or any shared goal rather than buying from a list. But platform fees make cash funds meaningfully more expensive than they appear.

Zola charges 2.5% per withdrawal on cash fund transfers. This fee is taken when you withdraw the money, not when it arrives. So:

- $1,000 in cash fund gifts: you receive $975
- $3,000 in cash fund gifts: you receive $2,925
- $5,000 in cash fund gifts: you receive $4,875

Some platforms market themselves as having lower fees. Hitchd, for example, has moved toward lower fee structures for cash registries. The point is not that cash registries are a bad deal - for most couples, the convenience is worth a 2.5% fee. The point is to include it in your TCO calculation rather than discovering it at withdrawal time.

## The Print Cost Math

If you order wedding invitations through an app-integrated stationery option (The Knot marketplace, Zola stationery, or Joy's print partner), you pay a convenience markup over what the same job would cost if you separated design from printing.

A common workflow that reduces print costs:

1. Design in Canva using a premium template ($15-$30 for a template you own)
2. Export print-ready files
3. Order from a direct-to-consumer print service (Printingforless, Moo, Smartpress, or similar)

For 100 invitation suites at a mid-range quality level, this approach typically runs $200-$350. The same quality level through an app-integrated stationery service runs $350-$550. The gap is roughly $150-$200 on a single order.

Over the course of an engagement, most couples have at least two print jobs: save-the-dates and invitations. The total savings from separating design and print can be $200-$400.

## When the Subscription Math Makes Sense

A paid monthly subscription at $20-$35/month is the right choice when:

- You are planning a complex wedding (multiple vendors, large guest count, significant budget to track)
- You value having a real budget ledger - one that distinguishes between estimates, deposits, scheduled payments, and actuals
- You want integrated vendor management with payment reminders
- You have a shorter engagement where you might not hit breakeven on a lifetime deal

If you are nine months out and planning a 100-person wedding with eight or more vendors and a $25,000+ budget, a paid subscription tool earns its cost in organized data and reduced administrative friction.

## When the Lifetime Deal Makes Sense

The lifetime deal at $100 is the right choice when:

- Your engagement is more than three months long (which is almost always)
- You want to avoid thinking about recurring billing during an already expensive year
- You want the lowest total cost over the full engagement

For the average 15-month engagement, the lifetime deal is $200 less than the Starter subscription and $425 less than the Pro subscription. There is no scenario where the lifetime deal costs more than the subscription for a normal-length engagement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long is the average wedding engagement?

The average US engagement lasts 13-18 months before the wedding date, based on wedding industry survey data from The Knot and similar sources. Some couples plan in under six months; others take two years or more. The typical planning window is long enough that a monthly subscription accrues significant cost compared to a one-time fee.

### Is it worth paying for a wedding planning app vs. using Google Sheets?

Google Sheets works well for couples who are comfortable building and maintaining structured spreadsheets. The limitation is not calculation power - spreadsheets can handle wedding budget tracking. The limitation is workflow: Sheets does not send you payment reminders when a vendor balance is due, does not integrate RSVP data into a seating tool, and does not have a mobile app designed for looking up vendor contact information while on a site visit. Whether those conveniences are worth $20-$35/month depends on how you plan and how complex your wedding is.

### Do free wedding apps really cost money through vendor advertising?

Yes, though the mechanism is indirect. The Knot and WeddingWire are advertising platforms. Vendors pay for placement. Those costs are part of vendor overhead and factor into pricing across the industry in markets where these platforms are dominant. You cannot easily calculate how many dollars of your catering bill pay for your caterer's WeddingWire advertising budget - but the economic relationship is real. The more direct question to ask: when a vendor appears at the top of The Knot search results, why are they there? It is advertising spend, not a quality ranking.

### What happens to my data if I cancel a paid wedding planning app?

This varies significantly by platform. A well-designed paid tool includes data export at any tier - you should be able to download your guest list as a CSV, your budget entries in a standard format, and any vendor records before or after canceling. Always verify this before committing months of data entry to any platform, free or paid. If a platform does not let you export your data, that is a meaningful switching cost built into the product intentionally.
