## What Wedsites Is and Where It Comes From

Wedsites launched in Australia and has expanded to serve couples in the US and UK. The product centers on wedding websites — clean templates, RSVP management, and guest communication. Planning tools (budget, checklist, vendor search) were added later as features to make the platform stickier, not as its founding premise.

That origin matters when you're evaluating it as a US planning tool. The vendor directory reflects an Australian-first build. US vendor coverage is thinner than platforms that were built from scratch for the American market. For couples in major US cities who rely on vendor discovery through their planning app, that gap is real.

## The Website-First Product Problem

Wedsites does the wedding website part well. The templates are minimal and refined — a different aesthetic from The Knot's busier designs. RSVP collection, meal selections, and guest communication work cleanly.

The planning tools follow the same pattern you see on most wedding website platforms: budget estimation by category, a checklist, and some vendor search functionality. These tools are functional for early-stage planning when you're building a rough picture of what you need and what it costs.

Where they fall short is execution-phase planning. A budget tool that shows "florals: $2,500 allocated" doesn't tell you whether you've paid the deposit, what the payment schedule is, or what your confirmed-and-paid total looks like at any given moment. For couples who want real financial tracking — actual vendor payments against a budget ceiling — that level of detail requires a different product.

## What Kaiplan Does Differently

Kaiplan was built around the planning workflow. Budget lines connect to specific vendors. Recording a vendor deposit updates the budget. Confirmed guest count updates per-head catering estimates. The seating chart knows who has RSVP'd yes.

The difference isn't just having more features — it's that the features connect to each other. Managing 12-15 vendors across a 12-18 month engagement with $30,000-$50,000 in spending requires those connections. A website builder with planning tools added on doesn't provide them.

Kaiplan starts at $20/month or $100 one-time for lifetime access. There's no free tier. The trade-off is explicit: you pay for a planning tool, and the tool is built to justify that payment.

## When Wedsites Makes Sense

If your primary need is a beautiful wedding website at low cost, Wedsites delivers that. The free tier is genuinely useful for building a website and collecting RSVPs. The paid tier at ~$49-79/year is among the more affordable options for a polished guest-facing site.

For couples planning outside the US — particularly in Australia or the UK — Wedsites has stronger vendor integrations for those markets.

Where Wedsites runs thin is for couples who want their planning tool to actually manage the planning: real payments, real vendor contracts, real budget numbers. Those couples tend to end up using Wedsites for the website portion and a spreadsheet (or a different app) for the planning portion. That's a reasonable approach, but it's two tools instead of one.

## The Honest Comparison

Wedsites is not a full planning app. It's a wedding website platform with planning features. If that distinction matters for your use case — and it does for couples in the execution phase of planning — it should drive your decision.
