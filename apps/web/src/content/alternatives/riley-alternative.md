## The Category Confusion Around Riley

Riley gets compared to consumer wedding planning apps because both appear in "wedding planning software" searches. The comparison doesn't quite land because they serve completely different users.

Riley is a B2B SaaS product for professional wedding planners. It manages the planner's workflow: proposals, client onboarding, vendor management, timelines, payment collection from clients, and business operations. A wedding planner might manage 20-30 clients in Riley simultaneously. The tool is designed around that professional workflow, not around a couple planning one wedding themselves.

Couples encounter Riley when their hired planner uses it. The couple gets a client portal — a view into their planner's workspace. They're not using Riley; their planner is.

## Who Riley Is Built For

Riley is genuinely good at its core use case. Professional planners who have found it frustrating to manage client communication across email, spreadsheets, and generic project management tools get real value from Riley's purpose-built features. Proposals, contracts, vendor management, and timelines are designed for the planner's professional workflow.

If you're a professional wedding planner evaluating business management tools, Riley is worth considering alongside Aisle Planner, Honeybook, and 17hats. But that's a different comparison than this page.

## What Self-Planning Couples Actually Need

A couple planning their own wedding needs different tools than a professional planner managing client weddings. The couple is the primary actor — they contact vendors, review contracts, track their own budget, manage their own guest list, and make all the decisions themselves.

The planning tool for that person needs to put them in direct control. Budget lines should be their budget lines. Vendor records should be their vendor records. The guest list, seating chart, and checklist should be their tools, not a view into someone else's business operations.

Kaiplan is built for that direct-control model. Budget, vendor management, guest list, and seating are all couple-facing features. At $20/month or $100 lifetime, the cost is structured for a couple paying for a wedding-planning tool, not for a business paying for client management software.

## The Make-or-Buy Decision on Planning

The decision Riley most directly influences isn't which planning app to use — it's whether to hire a professional planner. If you hire a planner who uses Riley, your planning tool experience is whatever client portal your planner sets up. If you plan without a professional planner, you need a direct-to-couple tool.

Full-service wedding planners charge 10-15% of the total budget. For a $40,000 wedding, that's $4,000-$6,000. Day-of coordinators are significantly less at $800-$2,000. Partial planning packages vary.

The financial trade-off is real. A couple planning their own wedding with Kaiplan at $100 lifetime is making a very different financial choice than hiring a professional planner at $5,000. The comparison isn't about tool quality — it's about whether the couple wants professional planning services at all.
