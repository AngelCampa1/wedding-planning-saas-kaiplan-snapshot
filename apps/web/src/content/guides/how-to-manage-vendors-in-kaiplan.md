## Why Vendor Management Is the Core of Wedding Planning

A wedding involves a lot of moving parts, but most of the complexity comes down to vendor management: finding vendors, getting quotes, comparing options, signing contracts, tracking deposits, managing multiple payment schedules, and staying on top of deadlines across 8-12 separate relationships — all while each vendor has different contract terms, payment policies, and communication preferences.

Most couples manage this through email threads, spreadsheet tabs, and memory. That works until it doesn't — usually when a payment due date gets missed, a contract term gets forgotten, or two vendors have conflicting requirements that nobody noticed until the week before the wedding.

Kaiplan's vendor management feature centralizes all of this information in one place. This guide walks through the full workflow: from adding your first vendor through confirming final payment.

---

## Step 1: Add Your First Vendor

Navigate to the **Vendors** section in Kaiplan and click **Add Vendor**.

You'll be prompted to fill in:

**Vendor category** — choose from the available categories (venue, catering, photography, videography, music/DJ, florals, attire, hair and makeup, cake/dessert, transportation, officiant, stationery, or other). Assigning a category connects the vendor to the right budget category automatically.

**Business name** — the name of the vendor's business.

**Contact name** — the specific person you're working with. Venues and catering companies often have multiple people on staff; record the name of your actual point of contact.

**Email address** — the email address for your contact. This becomes your reference point for outreach.

**Phone number** — mobile or direct line for your contact.

**Website** — optional, useful for reference.

**Booking status** — set this to where you currently stand:

- **Considering** — you've heard of them or gotten a referral, haven't reached out yet
- **Contacted** — you've sent an inquiry
- **Quote received** — you have a pricing proposal
- **Booked** — contract signed
- **Completed** — event has passed

**Price/Quote** — enter the quote you've received or the contracted amount if you've signed. If you have a range, enter the higher end.

Save the vendor. You've now created the record you'll build on as the relationship develops.

## Step 2: Enter Contract Details When You Book

When you move from quote to contract, update the vendor record with the actual signed amounts and payment terms.

**Contracted amount:** Update the price field to reflect the exact amount in your contract, not the original quote if it changed during negotiation.

**Contract notes:** This is where you record anything important from the contract that you'll need to reference later. Useful things to capture here:

- Start and end times covered by the contract
- What's included vs. what costs extra (additional hours, second shooter for photography, etc.)
- Cancellation and refund policy
- Vendor meal requirements (many contracts specify that you provide vendor meals at the reception)
- Any vendor-specific logistical requirements (load-in time, parking, dedicated outlet requirements for DJs, etc.)
- The contract signature date

You don't need to copy the entire contract into this field — just the details you'll need to reference quickly during planning.

**Confirmation number:** Some vendors provide a booking confirmation number when you sign. Record it here so you have it in one place if you ever need to reference it.

## Step 3: Set Up the Payment Schedule

Most wedding vendor contracts have at least two payments: a deposit at booking and a final balance due closer to the wedding. Some have three or more installments.

For each vendor, enter:

**Deposit amount** — the dollar amount of the deposit required at booking. If you've already paid it, enter the amount and mark it paid.

**Deposit due date** — when the deposit was or is due.

**Deposit paid status** — toggle this to paid once you've sent the payment.

**Balance amount** — the remaining amount after the deposit. For simple two-payment contracts, this is the contracted amount minus the deposit.

**Balance due date** — when the final balance is due. Many vendors require final payment 2-4 weeks before the event.

**Balance paid status** — toggle this to paid once you've sent the final payment.

**For vendors with multiple installments:** If a vendor has a three- or four-payment schedule, use the contract notes field to record the full payment schedule. Then track the two primary payments (deposit and final balance) in the main payment fields.

## Step 4: Monitor Payment Due Dates

Once you have payment due dates entered for your vendors, Kaiplan surfaces upcoming due dates on your dashboard and in the vendor list.

**Why this matters:** For most weddings, vendor final payments cluster in the 2-4 weeks before the wedding. This creates a period where you may have 6-10 large payments due within a short window. Missing a payment due date can put your booking at risk — some vendor contracts allow them to release a date if payment isn't received on time.

The vendor list view shows you payment status for every vendor at a glance. You can see:

- Which deposits are pending (upcoming due date, not yet paid)
- Which deposits are past due
- Which final balances are upcoming
- Which balances are past due

Check this view regularly as your wedding date approaches. Sort by payment due date to see the sequence of upcoming payments.

**Budget context:** Because the vendor manager feeds into the budget tracker, you can see both your upcoming payment obligations and your overall budget status in the same tool. This helps with cash flow planning — knowing that you have $4,500 in vendor balances due in the next 30 days means you can make sure that money is in the right account.

## Step 5: Compare Vendors in the Same Category Before Booking

Before committing to a vendor in any category, it's useful to have multiple options recorded so you can compare them systematically rather than relying on memory or scattered email threads.

**How to set up a comparison:**

1. Add all vendors you're considering in the same category with status "considering" or "quote received"
2. Enter their quoted prices
3. Use the contract notes field for each vendor to record your impressions, what's included vs. excluded in their quote, and anything relevant from your conversations
4. View the category in the vendor list to see all options side by side with prices

When you make a decision, update the chosen vendor's status to "booked" and either remove or mark the others as "not selected." This keeps your vendor list clean and your budget tracker accurate.

## Step 6: Track Vendor Communication Notes

The contract notes field serves double duty: it holds contract terms and also serves as a running log of anything important from your vendor conversations.

**Useful things to record over time:**

- "Spoke with Sarah 3/15 — she confirmed she can arrive at 11am for setup"
- "Caterer confirmed final headcount due by April 1"
- "DJ requires a 6-outlet power strip and 30-min load-in time"
- "Photographer said she'll send shot list template 8 weeks before wedding"
- "Venue requires a certificate of insurance from caterer — need to request"

These notes don't need to be formal. They just need to be somewhere findable when you need them six months from now. Your email inbox is not a reliable notes system for this kind of information — things get buried, threads get long, and context gets lost.

---

## Step 7: Handle Vendor Changes

Things change during the planning process. Vendors cancel, prices adjust, payment dates shift. Here's how to handle common scenarios:

**Vendor raises their price after quoting:** Update the amount in the vendor record. The budget tracker recalculates automatically. If the new price puts you over budget in that category, you'll see it immediately.

**Your vendor cancels or you need to cancel:** Update the booking status to reflect the situation and note the cancellation date and any refund received in the notes field. If you need to find a replacement, add the new vendor as a separate record.

**Contract amendment:** If you sign an amended contract with different terms or pricing, update the relevant fields and note the amendment date in the contract notes.

**Vendor merges or changes contact:** Update the contact information in the record. If a new person takes over your account (common with larger venues and catering companies), update the contact name and email so you're reaching out to the right person.

## A Practical Workflow for Vendor Management

The most effective way to use Kaiplan's vendor management is to update records in real time rather than in batches.

When you get off a call with a potential photographer, add them to Kaiplan before you open your next tab. When you receive a quote via email, enter it in the vendor record before you file the email. When you sign a contract, update the status and enter the payment schedule while the contract is in front of you.

The 2-3 minutes it takes to update a vendor record immediately after an interaction is much faster than trying to reconstruct the information from email threads and memory later.

At the peak of planning, you might have 15-20 vendor records in various stages. Keeping each one current means your dashboard gives you an accurate snapshot of where things stand: what's booked, what's pending, what payments are coming up, and how your total committed spend compares to your budget.
