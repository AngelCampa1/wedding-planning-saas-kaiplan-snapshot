## Before You Start: What to Know About The Knot's Data Export

The Knot does not make it easy to export your planning data. This is a common frustration for couples who want to switch tools — the platform isn't built to help you leave it.

Here's what you can and can't get out of The Knot:

**What you can export:**

- Guest list (CSV download available in the guest list section)

**What you cannot bulk-export:**

- Vendor contact information (no download option for your saved vendor list)
- Checklist tasks and completion status
- Budget tracker data
- Messages with vendors

**What this means for your migration:** Plan to manually copy vendor contact information from The Knot before you switch. If you have 10 vendors in The Knot, that's 10 records to screenshot or copy by hand. It's tedious, but it's the only way.

---

## Step 1: Export Your Guest List from The Knot

Before anything else, export your guest list. This is the one thing The Knot lets you do cleanly.

1. Log in to The Knot
2. Navigate to the **Guest List** section of your planning dashboard
3. Look for the **Export** or **Download** option (typically in the top area of the guest list view)
4. Download the CSV file

Save the file somewhere accessible (your Downloads folder or Google Drive). You'll use this in Kaiplan's import flow.

**Check what's in the export:** Open the CSV and verify it has the supported fields you want to keep: first name, last name, email, phone, party/group, dietary tags, and dietary notes. The Knot's export may include more or fewer columns depending on what data you've entered. Rename the headers you want Kaiplan to import to its supported field names before uploading. RSVP status is not part of the CSV import, so keep that column as a reference for manual updates after import.

---

## Step 2: Save Your Vendor Contact Information

This is the most manual part of the migration and there's no shortcut.

1. Go to the **Vendors** section in The Knot
2. For each vendor you have saved, record the following:
   - Business name
   - Contact name
   - Email address
   - Phone number
   - Website
   - Amount quoted or contracted
   - Any payment terms or notes you've recorded

The most efficient approach is to open a temporary Google Sheet and create a row for each vendor with these columns. You'll use this sheet as your source for re-entering data in Kaiplan.

**If you're pressed for time:** At minimum, save the email address for each vendor. Everything else can be reconstructed from their website or contract, but having direct contact emails means you're not starting from scratch on outreach.

**Save vendor messages:** The Knot's vendor messaging system stores communication between you and vendors. If you have important conversations there (specific requests, confirmed details, pricing discussions), either save those messages or note the key points before you close out The Knot as your primary tool.

---

## Step 3: Note Your Current Checklist Status

The Knot has a planning checklist with hundreds of tasks organized by timeline. You won't be able to export this checklist, but you can note which milestone categories you've completed.

Spend 5-10 minutes reviewing your Knot checklist and noting:

- Which major milestones are done (venue booked, save-the-dates sent, caterer contracted, etc.)
- Which categories have pending tasks you need to carry forward
- Any custom tasks you've added that you don't want to forget

Write this down or take a screenshot of each checklist section. You'll use this to set up your Kaiplan checklist.

---

## Step 4: Record Your Budget Data

The Knot's budget tracker stores estimates and actual amounts. You'll need to manually note this data before switching.

From The Knot's budget section, record:

- Your total wedding budget
- Any category-level budget allocations you've set
- Actual amounts entered for vendors you've booked

If you've been actively using The Knot's budget tracker, you may have a fair amount of data here. Budget category totals and individual vendor amounts are the most important to capture.

---

## Step 5: Create Your Kaiplan Account

Go to [kaiplan.app](https://kaiplan.app) and create your account. You can sign up with email or Google.

Once you're in, set your wedding date and total budget as described in the [getting started guide](/resources/guides/how-to-get-started-with-kaiplan).

---

## Step 6: Import Your Guest List into Kaiplan

Take the CSV you exported from The Knot and import it into Kaiplan:

1. Navigate to the **Guests** section
2. Click **Import**
3. Upload your CSV from The Knot
4. Confirm the CSV uses Kaiplan's supported field names
5. Run the import

Check the imported list to make sure everything looks correct. Then use your Knot export as a reference to update RSVP statuses in Kaiplan.

For a detailed walkthrough of the guest list import process, see the [guest list import guide](/resources/guides/how-to-import-guest-list-into-kaiplan).

---

## Step 7: Add Your Vendors to Kaiplan

Using the vendor information you saved in Step 2, add each vendor to Kaiplan:

1. Navigate to the **Vendors** section
2. For each vendor, click **Add Vendor** and fill in:
   - Vendor category
   - Business name and contact information
   - Booking status
   - Contracted or quoted amount
   - Deposit amount, due date, and whether it's been paid
   - Balance due date
   - Contract notes (any important terms or details)

If you have 10 vendors, this takes 15-30 minutes. Do it systematically — start with your booked vendors (venue, caterer, photographer, etc.) since their data is the most complete, then add vendors you're still evaluating.

**Pay attention to payment due dates.** If you have any deposits or balances due in the near term, enter those first so Kaiplan surfaces them as upcoming due dates right away.

---

## Step 8: Set Up Your Budget Categories

In Kaiplan's budget section, create categories that match the areas you were tracking in The Knot:

1. Go to the **Budget** section
2. Add category lines for each major spending area (venue, catering, photography, etc.)
3. Enter your budget allocation for each category based on what you had in The Knot

The categories don't need to match The Knot's categories exactly — use whatever breakdown makes sense for your specific wedding. Most couples use 8-12 categories.

Once you've added your vendors (Step 7), their amounts will populate into the relevant budget categories automatically based on which category you assigned each vendor to.

---

## Step 9: Configure Your Planning Checklist

In Kaiplan's checklist or timeline section, review the default milestone tasks and:

1. Mark tasks as complete that you've already finished (based on the notes you took in Step 3)
2. Add any custom tasks that were specific to your planning
3. Adjust target dates for upcoming tasks

You don't need to replicate The Knot's checklist exactly. Kaiplan's default checklist covers the major milestones. Use it as a prompt for what still needs to happen rather than trying to recreate every task from The Knot.

---

## What You Lose When You Leave The Knot

Be clear-eyed about what you're giving up:

**Vendor directory access:** The Knot's vendor directory is one of its most valuable features if you're still researching vendors. The directory is publicly accessible even without a planning account, but your saved vendors and any vendor search filters you've set up won't carry over.

**Vendor messaging history:** If you've had planning conversations through The Knot's messaging system, that history stays in The Knot. Save any important communication before you close out.

**Wedding website integration:** If you're using The Knot's wedding website builder, that stays on The Knot. You can keep your Knot wedding website active while using Kaiplan for planning management — you don't have to choose one or the other.

**Checklist format:** The Knot has a very detailed checklist with hundreds of tasks across many categories. Kaiplan's checklist is simpler. If you relied heavily on The Knot's checklist as a task management system, the transition may feel like a reduction in detail.

---

## The Hybrid Approach (Keep Both)

Many couples who migrate to Kaiplan for planning management don't fully delete their Knot accounts. Instead:

- **The Knot:** Public wedding website, RSVP forms for guests, vendor discovery research
- **Kaiplan:** Budget tracking, vendor management, guest list tracking, planning checklist

This hybrid approach works well because the tools solve different problems. The Knot's strengths are public-facing (wedding website, vendor discovery). Kaiplan's strengths are private planning management. Using both isn't redundant — they serve different purposes.

---

## After the Migration

Once your data is in Kaiplan, spend a few minutes verifying that everything looks right:

- Budget total matches what you had in The Knot
- All booked vendors are entered with correct amounts and payment status
- Guest list imported cleanly and RSVP statuses were updated from your export reference
- Key checklist milestones are marked complete

Then update your workflow: when new vendor quotes come in, enter them in Kaiplan. When an RSVP comes back, update it in Kaiplan. When a deposit goes out, mark it in Kaiplan.
