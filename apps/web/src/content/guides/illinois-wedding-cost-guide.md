## How much does a wedding in Illinois cost?

Illinois wedding costs are shaped almost entirely by whether you're planning in Chicago or outside it. Based on national data from The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study adjusted for Illinois's cost of living, the state-wide average is approximately $39,300. But that number reflects Chicago's pull on the statewide estimate.

Chicago is a major wedding market with high venue minimums, competitive vendor rates, and strong demand for peak-season dates. Couples planning in neighborhoods like River North, West Loop, or the Gold Coast, or at venues on the city's lakefront, often budget $50,000–$75,000.

Downstate Illinois is a different story. Peoria, Springfield, Champaign, and Rockford all have active wedding vendor markets with significantly lower pricing. Barn and winery venues in rural central and southern Illinois offer competitive rates in scenic settings.

Peak season in Illinois runs May through October, with June and September the most popular months. Winter weddings offer real savings but require indoor venue planning.

## Breaking down Illinois wedding costs

The table above shows typical ranges across vendor categories. Chicago-specific notes:

- **Venue minimums** in Chicago's premium neighborhoods run $20,000–$40,000 for weekend events. Compare that to downstate venues where a flat rental fee of $3,000–$6,000 with a bring-your-own-caterer option may be possible.
- **Photography** rates in Chicago are competitive for a major urban market. Many excellent Chicago photographers are active in the $3,500–$6,000 range.
- **Catering** in Chicago almost always includes a service charge of 20–22% plus tax, confirm the all-in per-person cost before comparing venues.

## How to reduce wedding costs in Illinois

**Go suburban or downstate.** Venues in the Chicago suburbs, DuPage County, Lake County, the Fox Valley, offer meaningful savings over city venues while staying within range of Chicago-based guests. Downstate venues save even more.

**Book January–March.** Chicago's wedding market slows significantly in winter. Venues are more negotiable on minimums and vendors have more availability.

**Look at micro-venues.** Chicago's restaurant and rooftop scene has expanded options for smaller weddings. Private dining rooms and rooftop spaces for 50–80 guests can often be booked at far lower cost than traditional wedding venues.

**Keep it under 100 guests.** Per-head catering in Chicago adds up fast. A guest list of 80 versus 130 is often the difference between a manageable budget and a budget that requires painful compromises elsewhere.
