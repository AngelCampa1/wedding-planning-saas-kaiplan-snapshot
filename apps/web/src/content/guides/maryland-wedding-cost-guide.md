## How much does a wedding in Maryland cost?

Maryland sits at the national average for wedding costs, but that average masks a significant divide between the state's different geographic markets.

Based on national data from The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study and regional market pricing, the estimated statewide average for a Maryland wedding is around $34,000 — nearly identical to the national average of $34,200. The problem with that number is that Maryland stretches from the DC suburbs to rural Appalachian counties, and the cost difference between those markets is enormous.

Northern Maryland — Montgomery County, Prince George's County, Frederick County, and Howard County — is effectively the DC wedding market. Vendors who base their pricing on DC area rates operate here, and venue fees, catering minimums, and service costs reflect it. A mid-tier 100-guest wedding in Bethesda or Columbia typically runs $40,000–$50,000.

Baltimore is a different story. Maryland's largest city has a growing wedding industry with independent venues and local vendors who price below DC corridor rates. A comparable 100-guest mid-tier wedding in Baltimore typically runs $25,000–$32,000.

The Eastern Shore is distinct again — waterfront Chesapeake Bay venues carry premiums for the location, but the rest of the Eastern Shore offers competitive rural and farm venue pricing below the state average.

Fall is peak season in Maryland. October is the most competitive month for venues statewide, particularly for waterfront and historic properties. If your date has flexibility, January and February offer the largest gap between fall pricing and available rates.

## Breaking down Maryland wedding costs

The table above breaks costs by the three main Maryland wedding markets. A few specifics:

- **DC corridor venue costs** include F&B minimums that can reach $18,000–$28,000 at high-end properties in Montgomery County. Confirm whether quoted venue fees include catering or are separate from it.
- **Eastern Shore waterfront venues** charge both a base venue fee and often a per-head catering minimum. The view premium is real — factor $4,000–$8,000 above inland venue equivalents.
- **Baltimore** has the most diverse venue market in the state, ranging from waterfront Inner Harbor venues at the higher end to converted industrial spaces in neighborhoods like Hampden or Station North that start considerably lower.

## Most expensive vs. most affordable Maryland markets

**Higher cost markets:**
- **Bethesda / Potomac / Chevy Chase (Montgomery County)** — The priciest Maryland market, drawing from DC's vendor pool entirely.
- **Annapolis** — Mid-tier pricing between DC and Baltimore, with waterfront premium venues along the Chesapeake and Severn River.
- **Eastern Shore waterfront (St. Michaels, Oxford, Rock Hall)** — Destination pricing for the most sought-after Chesapeake venues.

**More affordable markets:**
- **Baltimore city** — Strong independent vendor market, growing event space availability, and DC-free pricing.
- **Frederick** — Historic and walkable, with lower price floors than Montgomery County despite being in the DC commuter corridor.
- **Western Maryland (Hagerstown, Cumberland, Garrett County)** — Rural market with the state's lowest venue rates. Deep Creek Lake in Garrett County has resort pricing, but surrounding properties are affordable.
- **Southern Maryland (Prince Frederick, La Plata)** — Often overlooked, with farm and estate venues at mid-to-lower price points.

## How to save money on a Maryland wedding

**Choose Baltimore or Central Maryland over the DC suburbs.** If you're not specifically attached to a Northern Maryland location, moving your venue search to Baltimore saves $10,000–$18,000 on venue and catering alone at a 100-guest event. The vendor pool is strong, the neighborhoods offer interesting backdrops, and you're not paying DC labor rates.

**Book a winter date.** January and February are Maryland's clearest off-season months. Venues that charge $14,000 in October may price January Saturdays at $7,000–$9,000. Vendors have open calendars and are more negotiable. Maryland winters are cold but manageable, and indoor venues aren't impacted by weather.

**Look at the Eastern Shore for the Chesapeake feel without the resort premium.** The well-known Chesapeake Bay venues in St. Michaels and Oxford are booked and expensive. Smaller marinas, yacht clubs, and farm properties along the Chester River, Wye River, or Choptank River offer comparable water views with lower fees and more negotiating room.

**Avoid October Saturday demand.** Maryland's fall foliage and temperate October weather drive intense competition for that month's Saturdays. If you can shift to a Sunday or move to early November, availability improves and rates come down meaningfully.
