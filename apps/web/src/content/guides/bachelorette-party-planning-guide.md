## Who Plans It and Who Pays

This is the piece that causes drama, and it causes drama because people avoid talking about it directly.

The maid of honor typically takes the lead on planning. In practice, especially in large wedding parties, this often becomes a group effort. Someone has to make decisions, though. A committee of five people who all want input and no one who owns the final calls is how you end up with five months of group chat and no confirmed date.

Whoever is leading it should communicate clearly with the wedding party early: "I am planning this. Here is what I need from each of you by [date]." Clarity on leadership prevents the most common frustration, which is the maid of honor doing all the work and resenting it, or multiple people planning different things that conflict.

**On cost:**

The bride does not pay for her own party. This is a consistent norm. How her portion gets covered varies:

- Equal split among all guests (most common)
- The maid of honor pays the bride's portion; everyone else splits the remainder
- A designated larger contribution from the wedding party; smaller contribution from friends-only guests

Whatever the arrangement, name it before the first payment is made. "We are each putting in $X toward covering [bride]'s share" is a sentence worth saying out loud.

The most common post-event resentment: two people expected everyone to cover the bride's costs equally, and two people expected each person to just cover themselves. Someone felt like they overpaid; someone felt like they were asked to pay for something they did not agree to. This is fixable with one clear email upfront.

## Destination vs. Local

Neither is automatically better. The right choice depends on the group.

**Reasons to do a destination bachelorette:**

- The core people all genuinely have the flexibility and budget
- The bride wants a getaway experience, not just an activity
- You want more time together than a single evening allows
- The group is small (4-6 people) and logistics are manageable

**Reasons to stay local:**

- Financial situations vary significantly in the guest group
- Several guests have young children or demanding jobs that make overnight trips difficult
- The bride finds travel stressful close to the wedding
- You can design a full-day experience locally that is genuinely special

Popular destination options: Nashville, New Orleans, Charleston, Austin, Scottsdale, Miami, Napa. All have established bachelorette infrastructure with vendor packages built around groups. The abundance of options in these cities also means more price competition.

**The budget reality for destinations:**

Nashville for a long weekend, per person: $600-$1,000 including flights from major cities, hotel, meals, activities, and going out. This is not a low-cost option. If you have a guest who makes $40,000/year and is already spending $200 on a bridesmaid dress and $150 on a bridal shower gift, asking them for $800 more on top of that is a real ask.

## What Activities Actually Work for a Group

Activity selection is where bachelorette parties go wrong.

**Activities that tend to work well:**

- Private dining experiences (wine pairing dinner, multi-course tasting menu, chef's table). You are seated, everyone can talk, it scales from 6 to 16 people, and the bride's dietary restrictions are manageable.
- Spa day or partial spa day. Not everyone needs the same services. A morning at a spa with a mix of massages and facials gives people flexibility.
- Cooking or cocktail class. Interactive, limited to a few hours, and does not require everyone to drink heavily to have fun.
- Escape room. Works for groups of 6-10. Everyone participates regardless of how much they drink.

**Activities that often underdeliver:**

- Club table reservations. These depend entirely on group dynamics. If your group genuinely loves dancing and staying out until 2am, it works. If half the group has 7am flights and is there to be polite, it is miserable for everyone.
- Scavenger hunts that require interaction with strangers. Some guests love this; introverts find it exhausting.
- Activities that require physical fitness (axe throwing, escape rooms, physical challenges) without knowing everyone's physical situation in advance.

Ask the bride: "What does a good night look like for you?" Some brides want quiet and intimate. Some want loud and chaotic. Design for what she actually wants.

## Including People With Different Financial Situations

This is real and worth thinking through explicitly.

**The clearest approach:** Anchor the event to the most constrained budget in the group, and add optional upgrades. A dinner and one activity at $150 per person works for almost everyone. If some guests want to add a spa morning the next day, they can opt in for an additional cost without obligating the group.

**Accommodation strategy for destination trips:** Book a house or Airbnb that allows varied room splits. Private rooms cost more; shared rooms cost less. People choose based on their budget and comfort level. Do not assume everyone needs their own room.

**Be direct about costs:** Send one email that says: "This event will cost approximately $X per person, covering [list of what's included]. Please let me know by [date] if you're in. No pressure if it doesn't work for you financially." People appreciate directness over having to read between the lines.

**Give people an easy out:** Not everyone who feels stretched can bring themselves to say so. "No pressure if the budget doesn't work" makes it socially acceptable to decline. People who feel financially trapped but cannot say so will either not show up last minute (leaving you with a deposit gap) or show up resentful.

## Timeline: When to Plan What

**3-4 months before the bachelorette event:**
- Confirm the date with the bride
- Decide on destination vs. local
- Get a rough sense of who is invited and their availability

**2-3 months before:**
- Send save-the-dates or initial invitations with estimated cost
- Get RSVPs and financial commitments
- Book venue, hotel, flights (if destination)
- Confirm restaurant reservations for popular spots (these book out)

**1 month before:**
- Finalize itinerary
- Send detailed logistics to all attendees (hotel info, timing, dress code if any)
- Collect any pre-payments needed

**1 week before:**
- Confirm all bookings
- Send a final logistics email with everything in one place
- Handle any last-minute drops or additions

The bachelorette party should happen 1-3 months before the wedding. Close enough that it feels connected to the event, far enough that the bride is not drowning in final logistics. Two weeks before the wedding is too close. She is busy, probably stressed, and the last thing she needs is another thing to show up for.
