## The Honest Version

We built Kaiplan, so take this evaluation knowing there's an obvious conflict of interest. We'll try to be as direct as possible about what the tool does well and where it genuinely isn't the right fit. You can assess how honest we're being by comparing against what other tools offer and what users say after they've tried it.

The short version: Kaiplan is worth it if your primary needs are budget tracking, vendor management, guest list, RSVP, seating, and a public wedding website in a single tool without ads or vendor marketplace dynamics. It's not worth it if you need vendor discovery, registry management, native guest apps, or print invitations.

At $20-$35/month, or $100 lifetime, it's not a large financial commitment. The question isn't whether you can afford it. The question is whether it solves the problems you actually have.

---

## What Kaiplan Does Well

### Budget Tracking That Tracks Real Numbers

Most wedding apps have a feature called "budget tracker" that is actually an estimate calculator — you tell it your total budget and it shows you suggested percentage allocations by category. That's not budget tracking. That's math.

Kaiplan's budget tracker is a ledger. You enter actual vendor contract amounts, actual deposits paid, actual balances remaining, and actual payment due dates. The tracker shows you your real committed spend vs. your total budget in real time, not an estimate of what you might spend.

This distinction matters because wedding cost overruns almost always happen by accumulation rather than by a single big overshoot. A venue that comes in 15% over estimate, a photographer that's 20% higher than budgeted, a florist quote that doubles after the initial conversation — each individual overage feels manageable, but by the time you've committed to all of them the total is significantly over your number.

A real-time tracker that updates with every vendor amount shows you the cumulative impact before you've committed, not after.

### No Vendor Advertising

Kaiplan has no vendor marketplace, no sponsored vendor listings, and no vendor advertising revenue. We charge couples directly for the planning tool.

This is a deliberate choice that comes with a real tradeoff: Kaiplan cannot help you find vendors. You have to bring your own leads from referrals, online searches, Instagram, or other sources.

The benefit is that nothing in Kaiplan is designed to steer you toward specific vendors. When you're evaluating two photographers, the comparison you see in Kaiplan is based on the information you entered — their quotes, your notes from consultations, their pricing — not on which photographer paid more to be featured.

Ad-supported wedding platforms aren't bad, but their business model creates incentives that don't always align with helping you find the best vendor for your specific situation.

### Lifetime Plan at a Fixed Cost

The $100 one-time Lifetime plan is a genuine differentiator for couples planning a longer engagement. Most wedding tools are subscription-based, which means you're paying monthly from the moment you sign up until after your wedding. If you're engaged for 18 months, $35/month adds up to $630. The Lifetime plan costs $100.

Even at the Starter tier ($20/month), 18 months is $360. The $100 Lifetime plan pays back in roughly three months of Pro subscription equivalent.

For couples who know they have a long engagement, the math on the Lifetime plan is straightforward. See [the detailed breakdown](/pricing) for what's included at each tier.

### One Tool Instead of Three

The practical value of having budget, vendors, and guest list in one tool is that you maintain one set of data rather than keeping a vendor spreadsheet, a separate budget spreadsheet, and a guest list spreadsheet in sync.

This sounds minor but it's a real friction reduction. When you add a vendor in Kaiplan, their amount automatically flows into your budget. When you update their payment status, the budget total updates. You're not copying numbers between files.

## Where Kaiplan Falls Short

### No Vendor Discovery

If you don't have good vendor leads, Kaiplan cannot help you find them. There's no directory, no search, no reviews, no marketplace.

This is the biggest gap if you're in a market where you don't know where to start. The Knot and WeddingWire have millions of vendor listings with reviews. If you need a florist in a city you don't know well and you have no referrals, those platforms are genuinely useful for finding options.

Kaiplan is for after you have leads. It manages the relationships with vendors you've already found.

### Website Limits

Kaiplan includes a public wedding website and RSVP flow connected to your guest list. It is not a registry platform, custom-domain website builder, hotel block manager, or native guest mobile app.

Some couples still use Zola, Joy, The Knot, or a standalone website builder if they need deeper guest-facing website customization or registry-first workflows.

### No Registry

There is no gift registry feature in Kaiplan. Registry management is a different product category and one we haven't built.

### No Print or Digital Invitations

Kaiplan does not design, print, or send invitations. Print invitations require a separate vendor (Zola, Minted, Paperlust, Artifact Uprising, or a local print shop). Digital invitations and RSVP forms are handled by your wedding website tool.

### No Native Mobile App

Kaiplan runs as a Progressive Web App (PWA) in your browser. It's accessible from your phone via the browser and works well on mobile, but there's no dedicated app to download from the App Store or Google Play. For some people this matters; for others it doesn't.

## Who Kaiplan Is Best For

Kaiplan fits best for couples who:

**Have a defined budget they want to actively track.** If you're planning a wedding with a fixed number and need to see your running committed spend in real time, Kaiplan's tracker adds real value.

**Are managing 6 or more vendors.** Below that number, a spreadsheet is probably fine. Above that threshold, having vendor contact info, payment schedules, and budget data in one tool starts to save meaningful time.

**Have already found their vendor leads.** Kaiplan is for managing vendor relationships, not discovering vendors.

**Want to plan without ad-driven recommendations.** If you're bothered by the idea of a planning tool that earns revenue from the vendors it recommends to you, Kaiplan's direct-pay model is a clean alternative.

**Are planning a longer engagement (6+ months).** The Lifetime plan is the best financial case for Kaiplan. Couples with a short planning window (3 months or less) get less value from the recurring cost relative to the planning time.

## Who Should Consider Something Else

**If vendor discovery is your primary need:** The Knot and WeddingWire have millions of vendor listings. Kaiplan has none. If you're starting from scratch on vendor research, those platforms are genuinely useful at that stage.

**If you want everything in one place including registry and website:** Zola integrates registry, wedding website, and some planning features in one product. It's ad-supported (Zola earns from vendor partnerships and registry commissions), but if the integration matters more to you than the business model, that tradeoff might be right for you.

**If your guest list is under 40 people and you're comfortable with spreadsheets:** For a micro-wedding or elopement, a Google Sheet probably handles everything you need. Kaiplan adds the most value at higher complexity.

**If you're planning a wedding in 60-90 days:** The per-month value of the subscription model makes less sense for a very compressed planning window. At $20/month, three months is $60 — but you should also be realistic about whether you'll use all of the features in a 90-day sprint.

## The Bottom Line

Kaiplan is a focused, honest planning tool. It does budget tracking, vendor management, and guest list management well. It starts at $20-$35/month, or $100 once for Lifetime. It doesn't have ads or a vendor marketplace.

If those priorities align with what you need, it's worth the cost. If you need vendor discovery, a wedding website, or a registry, it isn't the right tool alone — but it can work alongside the tool that handles those things.
