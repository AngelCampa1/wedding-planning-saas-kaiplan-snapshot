## Why App Evaluations Go Wrong

Most couples pick a wedding planning app the same way they pick a restaurant on a first date: based on name recognition, a friend's recommendation, or whichever result appears first when they search. Then they spend three months with the tool before discovering that it can't do the one thing they actually needed it to do.

This happens because wedding apps are marketed on feature counts rather than feature depth. "Budget tracking, vendor management, guest list, checklist" sounds complete. But "budget tracking" can mean a calculator that shows you estimated percentage allocations, or it can mean a real ledger that tracks every contract, deposit, and payment due date. Those are different products with different utility for actually managing a wedding.

This guide is a framework for evaluating the depth behind the feature labels, not just whether a feature checkbox exists.

## The Five Dimensions That Actually Matter

### 1. Budget Tracking Depth

This is the dimension where the most variation exists between tools, and where the difference between good and bad implementation is most consequential.

**What to ask:**
- Can I record the actual contracted amount for each vendor?
- Can I record partial payments (deposits, installments) and see the remaining balance per vendor?
- Can I see my total committed spend versus total budget in real time?
- Does the tool show me upcoming payment due dates?
- Can I categorize actual spend to compare against my initial estimates by category?

**Minimum acceptable bar:** Record actual contracts, track deposits paid, show remaining balances and upcoming due dates. Anything less is an estimate calculator, not a budget tracker.

**Red flags:** Budget tool that only asks for total budget and shows percentage estimates. No way to record actual vendor amounts. No payment schedule tracking.

**Scoring (0-3):**
- 0: Estimate calculator only, no actual contract tracking
- 1: Can record vendor amounts but no payment tracking
- 2: Records amounts and tracks payments, no due date reminders
- 3: Full ledger with contracts, deposits, balances, due dates, and reminders

### 2. Vendor Management Features

Average wedding: 8-12 vendors. Each has a contact, a contract, a payment schedule, and a communication history. Managing that across email and memory is how vendor issues happen.

**What to ask:**
- Can I store vendor contact information in the tool?
- Can I track the status of each vendor (considering, contacted, booked)?
- Is there a place to note contract terms or attach documents?
- Can I record the payment schedule for each vendor?
- Can I compare vendors in the same category before deciding?

**Minimum acceptable bar:** Store contact info and booking status, record payment schedules, track which vendors are booked versus under consideration.

**Red flags:** No dedicated vendor section. Vendor tracking exists only as a tab inside the budget, without contact management. No ability to track pre-booking vendor conversations.

**Scoring (0-3):**
- 0: No vendor management
- 1: Basic contact storage only
- 2: Contacts, booking status, and payment tracking
- 3: Full vendor management with comparison, contracts, payments, and communications

### 3. Guest List Handling

Guest list and RSVP management varies significantly between tools. The minimum you need: a place to store guest names, addresses, and RSVP status. Better tools add meal preference tracking and table assignment.

**What to ask:**
- Can I import a guest list from a spreadsheet?
- Does it track RSVP status, meal preferences, and dietary restrictions?
- Can I assign guests to tables or track seating?
- Does it connect to a wedding website or RSVP form?
- Can I export the guest list for catering or venue use?

**Minimum acceptable bar:** Import, RSVP tracking, meal preferences, and export to CSV for vendor use.

**Red flags:** Guest list locked in the tool with no export. No meal preference field. RSVP only works if guests receive an email from the tool (some tools can't accommodate guests who RSVP by phone or mail).

**Scoring (0-3):**
- 0: No guest list feature or guest list is read-only
- 1: Basic list with RSVP status
- 2: Full RSVP with meal preferences, importable and exportable
- 3: Full RSVP plus seating, table management, and wedding website connection

### 4. Pricing Model and Commercial Conflicts

The business model of a wedding app determines what it's optimized for. This isn't a minor concern. It explains why features are deep in some areas and shallow in others.

**What to ask:**
- How does the app earn revenue?
- Does it have a vendor directory or marketplace? Are vendors paying for placement?
- Does it earn fees from vendor bookings or referrals?
- Does the app recommend specific vendors? If so, are those paid recommendations?

**Free tools supported by vendor advertising** earn from the vendors you might book. Their incentive is to surface vendors, generate leads, and facilitate bookings. Planning tools that make it easier to not book any particular vendor are not their priority.

**Paid tools without vendor revenue** earn from you directly. Their incentive is to build tools that make planning easier for the couple. Budget tracking depth, vendor management, and data export matter because they affect whether you renew or cancel.

This doesn't mean free tools are bad or paid tools are always better. It means you should understand what the tool is optimized for before relying on it.

**Scoring (0-3):**
- 0: Revenue primarily from vendor advertising and lead generation
- 1: Mixed model with some vendor partnerships and some direct revenue
- 2: Primarily subscription-funded with some vendor integrations
- 3: Fully couple-funded, no vendor revenue, no commercial conflicts

### 5. Data Portability

What happens to your planning data if you switch tools, cancel, or need to share information with a coordinator or venue?

**What to ask:**
- Can I export my budget as a CSV or PDF?
- Can I export my vendor list with contact information?
- Can I export my guest list in a format usable by a caterer or venue?
- If I cancel, how long do I have access to my data?
- Is there a way to share a read-only view with my partner or coordinator?

**Minimum acceptable bar:** Budget and vendor list exportable as CSV. Guest list exportable for use with catering.

**Red flags:** No export functionality. Data deleted immediately on cancellation. No way to share data with a coordinator without giving them full account access.

**Scoring (0-3):**
- 0: No export, data locked in the tool
- 1: PDF export only, no machine-readable format
- 2: CSV export for key data types
- 3: Full CSV/PDF export, sharing features, data accessible post-cancellation

## The Scoring Rubric

Score each tool on the five dimensions above (0-3 each, 15 points possible):

| Dimension | Weight | Your Score |
|---|---|---|
| Budget tracking depth | 3x | __ / 9 |
| Vendor management | 2x | __ / 6 |
| Guest list handling | 1x | __ / 3 |
| Pricing model / conflicts | 2x | __ / 6 |
| Data portability | 2x | __ / 6 |
| **Weighted total** | | **__ / 30** |

Weighting rationale: Budget tracking is weighted 3x because it's where the most expensive mistakes happen. Vendor management and pricing model/conflicts are weighted 2x because both significantly affect how useful the tool is for actual planning. Guest list and data portability are weighted 1x and 2x respectively because they matter but are less often the deciding factor.

A score of 20+ is a solid planning tool. 15-19 is adequate with notable gaps. Below 15 means you'll likely need supplemental tools.

## Testing Before You Commit

Before paying for any tool, run a 30-minute trial test:

1. Create a free account or start a trial
2. Add 3 test vendors with contact info, contract amounts, and payment schedules
3. Record two partial payments against one vendor and check if the balance updates correctly
4. Add 10 test guests with RSVP status and meal preferences
5. Try to export the vendor list and budget as a CSV
6. Look for upcoming payment reminders or due date visibility

If you can't complete all five steps within 30 minutes using only the tool's interface, that's a signal about how functional the tool actually is.

The goal isn't to find the perfect tool. It's to find a tool that handles the dimensions where mistakes are expensive, and to understand the tradeoffs clearly before you're three months into an engagement with your planning data locked inside.
