## Start With the Route, Not the Vehicle

Most couples think about wedding transportation backwards. They picture the vehicle first (a vintage Rolls, a classic Bentley, a party bus), then try to figure out logistics. The better approach is to map your route first, then figure out what vehicle makes sense for that route.

When we built Kaiplan, we noticed that transportation was one of the most common sources of day-of chaos. Not because couples skipped it, but because they booked vehicles without fully thinking through how many stops they actually had and how those stops interacted with their photo timeline.

Your wedding day transportation likely involves more legs than you realize:

- Getting ready location (hotel, family home, salon) to ceremony venue
- Ceremony venue to photo location (if it is a separate spot from the reception)
- Photo location to reception venue
- Reception venue back to hotel at end of night (for you and possibly guests)

Each of those legs has a time window. Vehicles with hourly minimums mean you pay for time between stops whether the car is moving or not. Map the full route, estimate realistic drive times with traffic buffers, then total the hours before you request quotes.

## Vehicle Types and What They Cost

### Limousines and Luxury Sedans

The classic choice for couples wanting something formal without the logistics of a larger vehicle. A stretch limousine or luxury sedan typically runs $500-$1,500 for wedding day service, usually a 4-6 hour minimum. Black car services in major markets charge $150-$250 per hour. Stretch limos in secondary markets often have flat-rate packages for 4-5 hours.

Capacity: standard stretch limos hold 8-10 passengers. SUV limos hold 12-14. Sedans hold 3 passengers in the back comfortably.

Best for: couples, small wedding parties (4-6 people), or transportation for immediate family members who are not riding with the main group.

Drawbacks: not ideal for larger wedding parties, and the interior photos with everyone crammed in a limo tend to look awkward for groups above 6-7 people.

### Party Buses

The practical choice for larger wedding parties. A party bus holds 16-30 passengers depending on the vehicle, keeps everyone together, and creates a shared experience before the ceremony. Costs run $1,000-$2,500 for 4-6 hours depending on bus size and market.

Capacity: smaller party buses hold 14-18. Larger ones hold 24-30.

Best for: wedding parties of 10-20 people, couples who want everyone traveling together, receptions where the party atmosphere starts in transit.

Drawbacks: size makes it harder to navigate in tight urban areas or venues with restricted parking. The vibe is more fun than formal, which may not match every wedding aesthetic.

### Vintage and Specialty Vehicles

Vintage cars (a 1960s Rolls-Royce, a 1950s Cadillac, a classic Bentley) are booked primarily for the photo and aesthetic value. You are not renting a vintage car because it is the most practical transport. You are renting it because you want those photos and because the vehicle itself is part of the visual story of your wedding.

Costs run $800-$2,500 for a typical 2-4 hour window. Many vintage vehicle vendors have strict policies about mileage, routes, and passenger counts because the cars are fragile. Some require a driver only (no chauffeur stop-and-go flexibility). Others limit the car to certain weather conditions.

Best for: couples with a specific visual aesthetic where the vehicle fits, or venues that photograph beautifully with a period-appropriate car in the frame.

Drawbacks: they break down more often than modern vehicles. The best vendors have backup plans. The worst do not. Ask directly about the contingency before you book.

### Guest Shuttle Service

A shuttle van or coach running between the main hotel block and the venue and back. Costs vary the most in this category: a single shuttle run with a 14-passenger van might cost $300-$500 for a short route. A full evening of multiple runs with a 40-passenger coach between a hotel 20 minutes from the venue can run $1,500-$2,000.

Costs are driven primarily by distance, hours of operation, and number of vehicles. Some couples run one shuttle at the start of the reception and one at the end. Others run continuous service throughout the evening.

Best for: receptions with limited parking, venues that are difficult to access by car, receptions with open bars where you want to reduce drunk driving, and guests who are elderly or unfamiliar with the area.

Not strictly necessary: if the venue has ample parking, guests are spread across multiple hotels, and the venue is easy to navigate with GPS, a shuttle may add cost without proportionate value.

## Booking Timeline

Transportation is one of the few vendor categories where vintage and specialty vehicles are legitimately harder to get than standard options. A stretch limousine company may have 6-8 vehicles available on any given Saturday. A vintage car vendor may have 2-3 cars that are actually road-reliable and safe to use for events.

General booking guidance:

Vintage cars and specialty vehicles should be booked 9-12 months before the wedding. If your heart is set on a specific type of classic car (a 1965 Ford Mustang convertible, a specific make or model), start looking even earlier and be prepared that your first choice may be unavailable.

Standard limousines and party buses should be booked 6 months before the wedding for peak season dates (May through October, holiday weekends). Three to four months is sometimes enough for off-peak dates, but availability tightens faster than most couples expect.

Guest shuttle services often have more flexibility, but popular charter bus companies in competitive markets fill their peak season calendars early. Book 4-5 months out to have good options.

Always confirm availability before falling in love with a specific vendor or vehicle. Time spent researching a company whose vehicles are already booked for your date is time you cannot get back.

## Planning the Logistics

The route and timing questions are where most couples underestimate complexity.

### Multiple Stop Logistics

A typical wedding day transportation scenario might look like this: bridesmaids get ready at a hotel and need to arrive at the ceremony venue 45 minutes before the ceremony. The couple has first-look photos at the ceremony venue, then portraits at a nearby park, then needs to arrive at the reception venue for cocktail hour.

If one vehicle is handling all of this, the timing has to be mapped very carefully. How long does it take to drive from the hotel to the ceremony venue? How long will portraits take? Is there traffic buffer built in?

The cleaner approach for complex timelines is multiple vehicles for different legs rather than trying to optimize one vehicle. A sedan takes the bridesmaids from the hotel to the venue. A party bus takes the full wedding party from the ceremony to portrait location to reception. A separate sedan or luxury car takes the couple alone during the transition moments where they want privacy.

This adds cost. But trying to do everything with one vehicle on a tight timeline is a reliable source of stress and late arrivals.

### Working With Your Photo Timeline

Talk to your photographer before booking transportation. Your photographer knows how much time portraits require and can tell you exactly how long the transition from ceremony to reception needs to be. A photographer who needs 90 minutes for portraits needs a vehicle that can accommodate that window without charging for idle time at a rate that breaks the budget.

Some couples try to book a limousine for just the couple and use the photographer's guidance to set a drop-off window. Others rent the party bus for a longer window so the wedding party can wait comfortably during extended portrait sessions. Either approach works. The mistake is booking the vehicle before talking to the photographer about the timeline.

### Guest Shuttle Logistics

If you are running a guest shuttle, the operational details matter:

Departure times: announce a specific shuttle departure time from the hotel (and post it on the wedding website). Give guests 15 minutes of buffer before the shuttle actually leaves. Announce the shuttle return times clearly so guests plan accordingly.

Multiple runs vs. single runs: a single run at the start of the reception and a single run at the end is simpler to communicate and manage. Multiple runs throughout the evening provide more flexibility but require guests to track the schedule. For receptions with 100+ guests, multiple runs may be necessary to avoid overcrowding.

Driver's phone number: give the shuttle driver's direct phone number to one point person from the wedding party. If guests get stuck or the driver is confused about a pickup location, there is a single person managing that communication.

## Questions to Ask Transportation Vendors

Before signing any transportation contract, these questions matter:

On the vehicle itself:
- Can I see the specific vehicle I am booking? (Not just a photo of the fleet.)
- What is the maintenance history and how recently was it serviced?
- For vintage cars: what is your backup plan if the vehicle cannot perform on the day?
- Is the vehicle climate controlled?

On the booking:
- What exactly is included in the quoted price? (Driver, fuel, tolls, gratuity?)
- What are the overtime rates if the service runs longer than booked?
- What is the cancellation and refund policy?
- What documentation do you carry? (Commercial vehicle insurance, chauffeur license, MC authority.)

On the driver:
- Who will be driving our vehicle? Can I speak with them before the day?
- What is their experience with weddings specifically?
- What is their protocol if there is a traffic emergency or breakdown?
- Are they available for a venue walkthrough or rehearsal run?

On logistics:
- What time can the driver arrive for load-in?
- Are there restrictions on the route (highway driving for a vintage car, for example)?
- How do you handle multiple stops on the timeline?

## Tipping Drivers

Tipping wedding drivers is standard practice and expected. The baseline is 15-20% of the total service cost in cash, given directly to the driver at the end of the service.

If the contract includes a gratuity line item, read it carefully. A 15% gratuity already included in a $1,500 bill means $225 is already accounted for. Tipping on top of that is a choice, not an obligation, but an extra $50 for exceptional service is never the wrong call.

Have cash ready before the wedding day. Do not rely on Venmo or the ability to pull cash from an ATM while you are in a suit or wedding dress running between venues. Give the cash envelope to a family member or the maid of honor to distribute to the driver at the appropriate time.

## Vendor Red Flags to Watch For

A few patterns in transportation vendors are worth being cautious about:

Showing you photos of vehicles they do not actually own. Some transportation companies operate as brokers, booking your service through third-party vendors without telling you. This is legal, but it means the vehicle in the photo may not be the vehicle that shows up. Ask directly: "Is this vehicle in your fleet or are you contracting it from another company?"

Vague insurance documentation. Any legitimate transportation company should be able to provide proof of commercial vehicle insurance on request. If they hedge, that is a problem.

Contracts that do not specify the vehicle by year, make, and model. A contract that says "luxury sedan" rather than "2024 Lincoln Continental, black" leaves room for a vehicle swap you will not know about until the day.

No backup plan for breakdowns. This matters most for vintage vehicles but applies to any fleet. Ask what happens if the vehicle has a mechanical issue. A good vendor has a clear answer. A bad vendor gets defensive.

## Putting the Budget Together

Total transportation budgets vary widely, but a typical breakdown for a couple doing it thoughtfully looks like this:

Couple's vehicle (limousine or luxury car, 5-6 hours): $800-$1,500. Wedding party vehicle (party bus, 4-5 hours): $1,200-$2,000. Guest shuttle (one hotel-to-venue round trip): $600-$1,200. Gratuity across all drivers: $300-$600.

That puts a realistic all-in transportation budget at $2,900-$5,300 for a couple who books all three. Couples who skip the guest shuttle and use a single luxury vehicle for just the couple and wedding party can land in the $1,500-$2,500 range.

Neither number is small. But transportation is one of those categories where the logistics failures (the car that is late, the vintage vehicle that breaks down, the couple who arrives at the reception exhausted from a scheduling scramble) have a direct impact on the experience of the day in a way that saved budget rarely compensates for. Book early, plan the route, and confirm the details in writing.
