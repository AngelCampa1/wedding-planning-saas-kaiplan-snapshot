## Before You Log In

We built Kaiplan because existing wedding planning tools were either too basic (a checklist app with no budget depth) or too bloated with vendor ads that pushed couples toward specific vendors whether or not those vendors fit their budget. Kaiplan is a focused planning tool: budget tracker, vendor manager, guest list, and timeline in one place, with no ads and no vendor marketplace.

Getting set up properly in the first session makes every subsequent planning session faster. This guide walks through each step of the initial setup so you're not starting from a blank slate.

**What you'll need before you begin:**

- Your wedding date (approximate is fine if you haven't locked a venue yet)
- A rough total budget figure (or at least a range)
- A list of any vendors you've already contacted or booked
- A rough guest count if you have one

If you don't have all of this, you can still set up your account — just leave fields blank and fill them in as you go.

---

## Step 1: Create Your Account

Go to [kaiplan.app](https://kaiplan.app) and click **Get Started**. You can sign up with an email address or continue with Google.

Once your account is created, you'll land on your planning dashboard. The dashboard gives you an overview of your current status across all planning areas: budget status, vendor count, guest count, and upcoming checklist tasks.

**Tip for couples:** If you and your partner both want access, sign up with one email and share the login, or invite your partner from within your account settings after setup. Having one shared account means you're both seeing the same data.

---

## Step 2: Set Your Wedding Date

The first thing to configure is your wedding date. Kaiplan uses this date to:

- Calculate how many days remain until your wedding
- Set default milestone dates in your planning checklist (e.g., "send invitations 8 weeks before wedding")
- Flag payment due dates that are approaching

Click the **Settings** or **Wedding Details** section and enter your wedding date. If you don't have a confirmed date yet, enter your target month and year — you can update this when you lock the venue.

You can also enter the venue name, city, and ceremony start time here. These aren't required for the planning tools to work, but filling them in makes the dashboard feel grounded in your actual wedding rather than generic placeholder data.

---

## Step 3: Set Your Total Budget

Navigate to the **Budget** section of your dashboard and enter your total wedding budget. This is the number you'll be tracking against as you add vendors and expenses.

A few things about how the budget tracker works:

- Your total budget is the starting figure. As you add vendors and log quotes, Kaiplan subtracts those amounts and shows you your remaining available budget.
- You can also set sub-budgets by category (venue, catering, photography, florals, etc.) if you want to track whether specific areas are on track.
- The tracker shows you the difference between your total budget and your total committed spend (contracts signed or deposits paid).

If you don't know your total budget yet, enter a working estimate. Couples often start with a round number like $25,000 or $40,000 and refine it as they get actual vendor quotes. The tracker is useful even with an estimated number because it forces you to see how quickly quotes add up against a fixed ceiling.

**Common mistake to avoid:** Don't wait until you've booked all your vendors to set up the budget tracker. The value of the tool is catching overages early, before you've committed. If you add vendors one by one and update the tracker as you go, you'll see in real time if a particular vendor choice is pushing you over budget.

---

## Step 4: Add Your First Vendors

Navigate to the **Vendors** section. Even if you haven't booked anyone yet, adding the vendors you're currently considering gives you a place to track quotes, contact info, and next steps.

For each vendor, you'll enter:

- **Vendor category** (venue, photographer, caterer, florist, DJ/band, hair and makeup, officiant, cake, transportation, etc.)
- **Business name and contact** (name, email, phone)
- **Quote or estimated price** (what they've told you or what you've estimated)
- **Booking status** (considering, contacted, quote received, booked)
- **Contract notes** (any details from your conversations or their contract)
- **Payment schedule** (deposit amount, deposit due date, balance due date)

You don't need to fill in every field for every vendor. At minimum, add the vendor name, category, and your best estimate of their price. That alone is enough to populate the budget tracker meaningfully.

**Start with your anchors:** The venue is usually the first vendor booked and the largest single expense. If you've already chosen a venue, add it first with the actual contracted amount, deposit paid, and balance due date. This alone gives you a realistic budget baseline to work from.

Once you have your venue entered, add other vendors in order of priority or booking status. Vendors you've already booked should have actual contract amounts. Vendors you're still evaluating can have estimated ranges.

---

## Step 5: Import or Build Your Guest List

Navigate to the **Guests** section to set up your guest list.

**Option A: Add guests manually**

Click **Add Guest** and enter each person individually. For each guest, you can record:

- First and last name
- Email address
- Phone number
- Party (who they're associated with)
- RSVP status (pending, invited, accepted, declined)
- Dietary tags or notes
- Linked plus-one guest records
- Notes

Manual entry works well for smaller guest lists or when you're building the list from scratch.

**Option B: Import from CSV**

If you already have a guest list in a spreadsheet (Google Sheets or Excel), you can export it as a CSV and import it directly into Kaiplan. Go to the Guests section and look for the **Import** option.

Your CSV will need columns for at minimum first name and last name. Optional supported columns include email, phone, side, group name, dietary tags, and dietary notes. Rename your spreadsheet headers to Kaiplan's supported field names before uploading.

After import, you can review each guest record and fill in any missing information.

**After your list is loaded:**

Once guests are in the system, you'll use the guest list for a few things:

- **RSVP tracking:** As invitations go out and responses come in, update each guest's RSVP status. The guest list shows you a running count of accepted vs. declined vs. no response.
- **Dietary notes:** If your caterer needs dietary context, you can record dietary tags or notes and include them in guest exports.
- **Plus-ones:** Add plus-ones as linked guest rows so they can have their own RSVP status and dietary notes.
- **Seating:** Some planning workflows use the guest list as the foundation for seating arrangement planning.

---

## Step 6: Configure Your Planning Checklist

Navigate to the **Checklist** or **Timeline** section. Kaiplan provides a default set of planning milestones organized by timeframe before your wedding: 12+ months out, 9-12 months, 6-9 months, 3-6 months, 1-3 months, and the final weeks.

The default checklist covers the major tasks most couples need to complete: booking the venue, sending save-the-dates, finalizing the caterer menu, ordering attire, scheduling hair and makeup trials, confirming vendor logistics, and so on.

**Customizing the checklist:**

You can:

- Mark tasks as complete as you finish them
- Add custom tasks that are specific to your wedding
- Adjust target dates for tasks based on your timeline
- Assign tasks to yourself or your partner

The checklist is most useful as a prompt for things you might forget rather than a rigid schedule. Wedding planning timelines vary significantly based on venue availability, vendor booking windows in your area, and your personal planning pace.

---

## Step 7: Connect the Pieces

Once you've completed the setup steps above, your dashboard should show:

- Total budget vs. total committed spend
- Number of vendors booked vs. total vendors you're tracking
- Guest list count and RSVP status summary
- Upcoming checklist tasks due in the next 30 days

If anything looks off, go back to the relevant section and fill in the missing data. The more accurate your data, the more useful the dashboard becomes as a real-time planning status view.

---

## What to Do in Your First Week

After initial setup, the most valuable thing you can do is keep updating the tool as planning progresses rather than letting it go stale.

**Habits that make Kaiplan useful:**

- When you get a vendor quote, log it in Kaiplan immediately rather than leaving it in your email inbox
- When you sign a vendor contract, update the status from "quote received" to "booked" and enter the actual contracted amount and deposit terms
- When a guest RSVPs, update their record
- When you complete a checklist task, mark it done

Kaiplan is not a tool that does planning for you. It's a tool that keeps your planning information organized and surfaced in one place so you're not managing vendor contracts in email, budget in a spreadsheet, and guest list in a separate doc. The value compounds as you use it consistently.
